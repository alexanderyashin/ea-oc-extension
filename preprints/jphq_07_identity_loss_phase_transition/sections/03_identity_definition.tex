\section{Enterprise Identity as Structure}

Enterprise identity is defined here as a structural property of the continuum
$K_{\mathrm{EA}}$, not as a narrative, symbolic, or descriptive label.

\begin{definition}[Identity-Supporting Subspace]
An \emph{identity-supporting subspace} is a subset
$\Lambda_I \subset \Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Lambda_I$ is admissible, i.e.\ $\Lambda_I \cap \partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}} = \varnothing$;
  \item there exists a non-empty set of cycles $C_I \subset C_{\mathrm{EA}}$
        whose trajectories are entirely contained within $\Lambda_I$;
  \item $\Lambda_I$ is invariant under the admissible flows $J_{\mathrm{EA}}$
        generated by these cycles.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Enterprise Identity]
Enterprise identity is the equivalence class of admissible states of
$K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ supported by an identity-supporting subspace $\Lambda_I$ and its
associated cycle set $C_I$. Two admissible states are identity-equivalent if they
belong to the same $\Lambda_I$ and are connected by trajectories generated by
$C_I$.
\end{definition}

This definition is purely structural. Identity is not an attribute added to the
enterprise, nor a property inferred from description or observation. It is an
emergent constraint on admissible motion within $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$, enforced
by the persistence of specific invariant cycles.
