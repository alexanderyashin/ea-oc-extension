\section{Identity Loss as Phase Transition}

We now formalize enterprise identity loss as a phase transition internal to
the continuum $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

\begin{theorem}[Identity Loss Transition]
Let $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ admit an identity-supporting subspace $\Lambda_I$ with
associated cycle set $C_I$. Identity loss occurs when system evolution causes
$\Lambda_I$ to lose admissibility or its supporting cycles to be extinguished,
while the admissible state space $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ remains non-empty.
Formally, identity loss is characterized by:
\[
\Lambda_I \cap \partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}} \neq \varnothing
\quad \text{or} \quad
C_I = \varnothing,
\]
with $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}} \neq \varnothing$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Identity Loss vs.\ Organizational Failure]
Identity loss is not equivalent to organizational failure. Identity loss
corresponds to loss of admissibility or extinction of the identity-supporting
subspace $\Lambda_I$, whereas organizational failure requires collapse of
$\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ or vanishing of the continuum integrity measure
$k_{\mathrm{EA}} \rightarrow 0$.
\end{proposition}

The transition defined above satisfies the criteria of a phase transition in the
OC sense: it involves threshold crossing, loss of admissibility of a structural
component, and a discontinuous change in the set of invariant cycles, without
necessitating collapse of the enterprise continuum as a whole.
