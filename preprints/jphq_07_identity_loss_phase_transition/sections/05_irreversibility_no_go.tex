\section{Irreversibility and No-Go Results}

We formalize irreversibility of identity loss as the structural impossibility of
reconstituting an identity-supporting subspace $\Lambda_I$ and its associated
cycle set $C_I$ without introducing new admissible structure, which is excluded
by assumption.

\begin{definition}[Identity Reconstitution]
Given a continuum $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ that has undergone identity loss, an
\emph{identity reconstitution} would consist in the existence of a (possibly
new) identity-supporting subspace
$\Lambda'_I \subset \Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ together with a non-empty cycle set
$C'_I \subset C_{\mathrm{EA}}$ such that all trajectories generated by $C'_I$
remain entirely within $\Lambda'_I$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[No-Go for Identity Reconstitution under Cycle Non-Regenerability]
Assume that after identity loss the enterprise continuum satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cycle extinction:} $C_I = \varnothing$ for the pre-loss identity;
  \item \textbf{Non-regenerability:} no admissible dynamics in
        $\mathrm{ETS}.K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ can generate new cycles in the
        identity-relevant class without introducing additional admissible
        structure (new axes, state variables, or threshold relations).
\end{enumerate}
Then identity reconstitution is impossible within the closed dynamics of
$K_{\mathrm{EA}}$: there exists no identity-supporting subspace
$\Lambda'_I \subset \Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ with an associated non-empty cycle set
$C'_I \neq \varnothing$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Irreversibility via Boundary Attachment]
If identity loss occurs through boundary attachment,
$\Lambda_I \cap \partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}} \neq \varnothing$, and if the
post-loss admissible state space contains no subspace that is both bounded away
from $\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ and invariant under a non-empty cycle set,
then identity loss is irreversible within $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$.
\end{proposition}

These results express irreversibility as a structural consequence of
(i) extinction of identity-supporting cycles and
(ii) closure of the admissible dynamics. They do not imply organizational
failure: the admissible state space $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ may remain non-empty
and the continuum integrity measure $k_{\mathrm{EA}}$ may remain positive while
identity-supporting structure is absent.
