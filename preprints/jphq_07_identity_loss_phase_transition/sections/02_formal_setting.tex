\section{Formal Setting}

We operate strictly within the Ontology of Continua (OC) and the executable
specification $\mathrm{ETS}.K_{\mathrm{EA}}$. No new primitives, operators, or
semantic reinterpretations are introduced.

An enterprise is modeled as a continuum
\[
K_{\mathrm{EA}} =
(\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}, \partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}},
A_{\mathrm{EA}}, P_{\mathrm{EA}}, \Theta_{\mathrm{EA}},
J_{\mathrm{EA}}, C_{\mathrm{EA}}, k_{\mathrm{EA}}, \tau_{\mathrm{EA}}),
\]
where $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ denotes the admissible state space,
$\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ its boundary,
$A_{\mathrm{EA}}$ the set of structural axes,
$P_{\mathrm{EA}}$ the associated potentials,
$\Theta_{\mathrm{EA}}$ the set of thresholds,
$J_{\mathrm{EA}}$ admissible flows,
$C_{\mathrm{EA}}$ the set of internal cycles,
$k_{\mathrm{EA}}$ the measure of continuum integrity, and
$\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$ the internal structural time.

Phase logic is inherited without modification from OC and ETS and distinguishes
between admissible operation, inertia, collapse, and stop. In particular,
\emph{organizational failure} refers to loss of admissibility of
$\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ or collapse of $k_{\mathrm{EA}}$ to zero. The present work
investigates a strictly weaker phenomenon that may occur while
$\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}} \neq \varnothing$.

All statements in what follows are structural. They refer exclusively to
admissibility, thresholds, cycles, and invariants internal to
$K_{\mathrm{EA}}$, and make no reference to narrative, cultural, psychological,
or managerial interpretations.
