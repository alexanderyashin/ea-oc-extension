\section{Problem statement}

This paper addresses a strictly formal question concerning the
\emph{enterprise-as-continuum} model as defined within the Ontology of
Continua (OC Core) and its executable specification ETS.K\_EA.

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{Frozen research question (Q10).}
\begin{quote}
Under what conditions is the enterprise-as-continuum model falsifiable,
and where are its formal limits?
\end{quote}

\medskip

The question is neither empirical nor predictive and carries no
managerial, operational, or normative intent.
It does not concern the usefulness, effectiveness, or practical success
of the model.
Instead, it asks whether the model admits internally well-defined
conditions under which it can be shown to be inconsistent, untenable, or
inapplicable \emph{within its own formal interface}.

\medskip

In this paper, \emph{falsifiability} is understood in a narrow,
model-internal sense.
An enterprise-as-continuum model instance is falsified if and only if
there exists an admissible observation statement that cannot be embedded
into any valid realization of the enterprise continuum \(K_{EA}\) without
violating at least one axiom, invariant, threshold, or structural
constraint imposed by OC Core and ETS.K\_EA.
No appeal to external theories, empirical performance measures, case
studies, or interpretive frameworks is permitted.

\medskip

The problem therefore decomposes into three logically prior questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item What constitutes an admissible observation statement within the
        formal vocabulary and constraints of the enterprise-as-continuum
        model?
  \item Under which conditions does such an admissible observation
        logically exclude \emph{all} admissible realizations of
        \(K_{EA}\)?
  \item Which apparent counterexamples or failure modes lie outside the
        admissibility domain of the model and must therefore be classified
        as non-falsifying by construction?
\end{enumerate}

\medskip

Clarifying these distinctions is necessary to prevent category errors
that conflate formal falsification with empirical mismatch, incomplete
observability, inference underdetermination, or logical undecidability.
In particular, the paper draws a strict separation between
\emph{falsification}, \emph{non-identifiability}, and the \emph{STOP}
outcome, the latter being treated as a first-class, explicitly
non-falsifying diagnostic result.

\medskip

The remainder of the paper formalizes necessary and sufficient conditions
for internal falsifiability, delineates the formal limits of applicability
of the enterprise-as-continuum model, and states all required non-claims
to preserve epistemic closure and logical discipline.
