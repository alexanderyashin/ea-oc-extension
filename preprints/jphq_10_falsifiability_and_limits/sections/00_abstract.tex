\begin{abstract}
This paper provides a strictly formal analysis of the falsifiability of the
enterprise-as-continuum model as defined within the Ontology of Continua
(OC Core) and its executable specification ETS.K\_EA~\cite{occore,etsk_ea}.
The investigation is confined to the model’s internal logical interface and
deliberately excludes empirical, managerial, predictive, or policy-oriented
interpretations.

Falsifiability is defined as an internal property of the formal system, in
explicit distinction from empirical falsification criteria in the classical
sense~\cite{popper1959logic}.
An admissible observation \(O\) is said to falsify the model if and only if
it entails that no admissible model realization exists that is compatible
with \(O\) in the state space \(\Omega(K_{EA})\); equivalently, the
observation forces internal inconsistency with at least one mandatory
invariant, threshold, or structural constraint that defines
\(\Omega(K_{EA})\).
Within this setting, the paper derives a precise characterization of the
necessary and sufficient conditions under which such internal falsification
occurs.

The analysis further draws sharp boundaries between falsification and a
range of formally distinct phenomena, including non-identifiability,
underdetermination, logical undecidability, and the \emph{STOP} outcome.
Undecidability is treated as a limitation of inference rather than a form
of falsification, in line with classical results in formal logic and
computability theory~\cite{church1936unsolvable,turing1937computable,godel1931incompleteness}.
STOP is characterized as a first-class diagnostic termination condition
that signals the exhaustion of admissible inference paths under the given
observation interface, rather than a failure or refutation of the model.

The result is a restrictive and fully explicit account of what the
enterprise-as-continuum model can, and cannot, be falsified by.
Falsification is possible in principle, but only through demonstrable
internal inconsistency; all other apparent failure modes are classified as
non-falsifying by construction.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} enterprise continuum; ontology of continua;
falsifiability; formal limits; admissibility; STOP
