\begin{abstract}
This paper provides a formal analysis of the falsifiability of the
enterprise-as-continuum model as defined within the Ontology of Continua
(OC Core) and its executable specification ETS.K\_EA.
The investigation is strictly confined to the internal logical structure
of the model and deliberately excludes empirical, managerial, predictive,
or policy-oriented interpretations.

Falsifiability is defined as a purely internal property of the formal
system.
An admissible observation is said to falsify the model if and only if it
logically entails the non-existence of any admissible model realization in
the state space \(\Omega(K_{EA})\), by contradicting at least one mandatory
invariant, threshold, or structural constraint.
Necessary and sufficient conditions for such internal falsification are
formulated and proven at the level of structural existence.

The analysis further establishes sharp boundaries between falsification and
a range of formally distinct phenomena, including non-identifiability,
underdetermination, logical undecidability, and the \emph{STOP} outcome.
STOP is shown to be a first-class diagnostic termination condition that
signals the exhaustion of admissible inference paths, rather than a failure
or refutation of the model.

The result is a precise and restrictive characterization of what the
enterprise-as-continuum model can, and cannot, be falsified by.
Falsification is possible in principle, but only through demonstrable
internal inconsistency; all other apparent failure modes are explicitly
classified as non-falsifying by construction.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} enterprise continuum; ontology of continua;
falsifiability; formal limits; admissibility; STOP
