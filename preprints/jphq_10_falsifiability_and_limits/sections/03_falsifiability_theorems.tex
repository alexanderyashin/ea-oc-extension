\section{Falsifiability criteria}
\label{sec:falsifiability-criteria}

This section formulates criteria under which the
enterprise-as-continuum model is formally falsifiable.
All statements are strictly internal to the Ontology of Continua (OC Core)
and its executable specification ETS.K\_EA.
No empirical, managerial, predictive, or external interpretation is
implied or required.

\medskip

Falsifiability is defined exclusively in terms of compatibility between
admissible observations and admissible model realizations in the state
space \(\Omega(K_{EA})\).
In particular, falsification is a statement about the non-existence of any
compatible realization and not about the behavior, success, or termination
of a diagnostic procedure.

\medskip

\begin{definition}[Internal falsification]
An admissible observation \(O\) \emph{internally falsifies} the
enterprise-as-continuum model if and only if there exists no model
realization
\[
s \in \Omega(K_{EA})
\]
such that \(O\) is compatible with \(s\), i.e., such that all statements in
\(O\) can be embedded into \(s\) without violating any mandatory invariant,
threshold, or structural constraint of the model.
\end{definition}

\medskip

\begin{theorem}[Necessary condition for falsifiability]
\label{thm:necessary-falsifiability}
If an admissible observation \(O\) is compatible with at least one model
realization \(s \in \Omega(K_{EA})\), then \(O\) does not falsify the
enterprise-as-continuum model.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition, internal falsification requires the absence of any
compatible model realization.
The existence of at least one realization
\(s \in \Omega(K_{EA})\) that is compatible with \(O\) therefore excludes
falsification.
\end{proof}

\medskip

\begin{theorem}[Sufficient condition for falsifiability]
\label{thm:sufficient-falsifiability}
If an admissible observation \(O\) logically implies inconsistency with at
least one invariant, threshold, or structural constraint that is mandatory
for all
\(s \in \Omega(K_{EA})\),
then \(O\) falsifies the enterprise-as-continuum model.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All admissible model realizations are defined as elements of
\(\Omega(K_{EA})\) satisfying the full set of mandatory constraints.
If \(O\) implies inconsistency with any such constraint, then no admissible
realization can exist that is compatible with \(O\).
By definition, this entails internal falsification of the model.
\end{proof}

\medskip

\begin{proposition}[Non-falsifying observations]
\label{prop:nonfalsifying}
Admissible observations that lead to non-identifiability, underdetermined
state reconstruction, or the coexistence of multiple compatible model
realizations do not constitute falsification.
Such outcomes indicate epistemic limitation rather than internal
inconsistency of the model.
\end{proposition}

\medskip

\begin{proposition}[STOP is not falsification]
\label{prop:stop-not-falsification}
The occurrence of a \emph{STOP} outcome, defined as the formal impossibility
of continuing diagnosis without violating admissibility constraints, is a
non-falsifying result.
STOP reflects the exhaustion of admissible inference paths under the given
observation interface and does not invalidate the
enterprise-as-continuum model.
\end{proposition}

\medskip

\begin{remark}
Falsifiability in this framework is strictly weaker than decidability and
strictly stronger than empirical refutation.
It occupies a narrow logical interval defined solely by the internal
consistency of admissible observations with the formal state space
\(\Omega(K_{EA})\).
Any outcome that does not force the set of compatible model realizations to
be empty is explicitly classified as non-falsifying.
\end{remark}
