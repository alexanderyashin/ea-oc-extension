\section{Conclusion}

This paper has established a precise and strictly internal notion of
falsifiability for the enterprise-as-continuum model formulated within the
Ontology of Continua (OC Core) and its executable specification ETS.K\_EA.

\medskip

Falsifiability is shown to be neither empirical nor operational in the
classical sense.
It is defined exclusively as an internal logical property: an admissible
observation falsifies the model if and only if it renders the set of
compatible model realizations \(\Omega(K_{EA})\) empty by violating at least
one mandatory invariant, threshold, or structural constraint.
This criterion is both necessary and sufficient.

\medskip

The analysis explicitly separates falsification from several neighboring
phenomena that are often conflated with it.
Non-identifiability, underdetermination, and the coexistence of multiple
compatible realizations are admissible outcomes and do not constitute
falsification.
Likewise, logical undecidability reflects limits of inference rather than
inconsistency of the model.
The \emph{STOP} outcome is formally characterized as a first-class
diagnostic termination condition marking the exhaustion of admissible
inference paths, not a failure or refutation of the model.

\medskip

Admissibility is shown to be logically prior to falsifiability.
Only observations expressible within the formal vocabulary and constraints
of ETS.K\_EA can participate in falsification.
Inputs outside this domain are excluded before any falsifiability question
arises.

\medskip

Taken together, these results place the enterprise-as-continuum model in a
narrow and well-defined logical position.
The model is falsifiable in principle, but only through internal
inconsistency.
All other apparent failure modes—ambiguity, undecidability, diagnostic
termination, or external disagreement—are explicitly classified as
non-falsifying by design.

\medskip

This closure completes the formal characterization of what it means for the
enterprise-as-continuum model to be falsifiable, what it explicitly does
not claim, and why these boundaries are necessary for logical coherence.
No interpretive or prescriptive conclusions are implied beyond the formal
scope established here.
