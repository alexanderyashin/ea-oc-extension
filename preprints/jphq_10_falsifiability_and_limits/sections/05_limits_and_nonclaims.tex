\section{Limits and explicit non-claims}

This section delineates the formal limits of the
enterprise-as-continuum model and states explicit non-claims.
These limits are intrinsic to the Ontology of Continua (OC Core) and the
executable specification ETS.K\_EA.
They follow necessarily from the structure of the formalism and do not
constitute weaknesses, failures, or empirical shortcomings of the model.
Rather, they specify the precise boundary of what the model does and does
not assert within its formal interface.

\medskip

\begin{nonclaim}[No empirical falsification]
The enterprise-as-continuum model does not treat empirical mismatch as a
criterion of falsification within its formal interface.
Disagreement with empirical data, measurements, case descriptions, or
organizational narratives does not, by itself, constitute falsification.
Only admissible observations that are expressible within the formal
observation language of ETS.K\_EA and that entail internal inconsistency
with mandatory axioms, invariants, thresholds, or structural constraints
can give rise to falsification.
\end{nonclaim}

\medskip

\begin{nonclaim}[No falsification by non-identifiability]
The inability to uniquely reconstruct a model realization from admissible
observations does not falsify the model.
Underdetermination, ambiguity, partial observability, and the coexistence
of multiple compatible realizations in \(\Omega(K_{EA})\) are formally
admissible outcomes.
Such situations reflect limits of identifiability rather than internal
inconsistency of the formal system.
\end{nonclaim}

\medskip

\begin{nonclaim}[No falsification by undecidability]
Logical undecidability of a query over the enterprise state space
\(\Omega(K_{EA})\) does not imply falsification.
Undecidability reflects limits of inference, computation, or expressive
power within the formal system and does not entail violation of axioms,
invariants, thresholds, or structural constraints that define
\(\Omega(K_{EA})\).
\end{nonclaim}

\medskip

\begin{nonclaim}[STOP is not model failure]
A \emph{STOP} outcome, defined as the formal impossibility of continuing
diagnosis without violating mandatory model constraints, is a boundary
condition of admissible inference.
It indicates exhaustion of admissible inference paths under the given
observation interface and is explicitly classified as a non-falsifying
result.
STOP does not assert the non-existence of admissible model realizations and
does not constitute a statement about internal inconsistency of the model.
\end{nonclaim}

\medskip

\begin{nonclaim}[No claims beyond the OC/ETS domain]
The enterprise-as-continuum model makes no claims about phenomena,
entities, relations, or causal mechanisms that are not representable within
the formal vocabulary of OC Core and ETS.K\_EA.
Statements formulated outside this domain are neither supported,
predicted, explained, nor contradicted by the model and are formally out
of scope.
\end{nonclaim}

\medskip

\begin{remark}
Taken together, these limits define a sharp boundary between
falsification, non-applicability, and epistemic limitation.
Falsification occurs exclusively through internal inconsistency with the
formal state space \(\Omega(K_{EA})\).
All other apparent failure modes are excluded by construction and
constitute well-defined, non-falsifying outcomes of the formalism.
\end{remark}
