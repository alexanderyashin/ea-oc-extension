\section{Introduction and Context}
This preprint investigates the formal limits of intervention in enterprises
modeled as structural continua under the Ontology of Continua (OC Core)
framework and its executable-closed enterprise specification (ETS.K\_EA).
Rather than asking how interventions \emph{should} be performed, we ask where
intervention is structurally impossible, self-defeating, or phase-destructive,
independent of intent, competence, or governance quality.

The guiding thesis is negative but precise: when admissibility is defined by
non-negotiable threshold systems and bounded state spaces, certain classes of
intervention are provably constrained. These limits are structural, not
managerial or psychological.

\section{Discussion and Implications}
The results formalize a class of ``no-free'' constraints on intervention that
are invisible to optimization-based or governance-centric analyses.
They clarify why certain transformation programs fail even under competent
execution: the failure mode is often structural, not operational.

The analysis also delineates a sharp boundary between safe interior dynamics
and boundary-confronting actions that necessarily trade continuity for reach.

\section{Conclusion}
We have shown that, under OC/ETS-consistent continuum constraints, intervention
across admissibility boundaries cannot simultaneously preserve thresholds,
continuity, and phase.
This establishes a formal ceiling on what intervention can achieve and provides
explicit falsifiability criteria for refuting the framework.
