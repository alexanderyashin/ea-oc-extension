\section{Intervention Operator}

\subsection{Definition}

\begin{definition}[Intervention operator]
An \emph{intervention} is a (possibly partial) operator
\[
\mathcal{I}:\Dom(\mathcal{I})\subseteq \Omega \to \Omega,
\]
interpreted as a state update $\omega \mapsto \mathcal{I}(\omega)$.

It is \emph{$\Theta$-preserving} if for every $\omega\in\Dom(\mathcal{I})$,
\[
\ThetaSys(\omega)\le 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \ThetaSys(\mathcal{I}(\omega))\le 0.
\]
\end{definition}

We intentionally do \emph{not} attach objective functions, optimality,
governance, or control semantics to $\mathcal{I}$.

\subsection{Boundary-crossing action}

\begin{definition}[Acts across the boundary]
An intervention $\mathcal{I}$ \emph{acts across} $\partial\Omega$ if there exists
$\omega\in\Dom(\mathcal{I})$ such that either
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\omega$ is in a neighborhood of the boundary and the image traverses the
boundary in the ambient space, i.e. $\omega \in \Cl(\Omega)\cap U$ with $U$
meeting both admissible and inadmissible states, and $\mathcal{I}$ changes the
state in a way that would be inadmissible without active constraint handling; or
\item equivalently for our purposes: $\mathcal{I}$ is not representable as a
restriction of an automorphism of $\Omega$ that leaves $\partial\Omega$
invariant.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Intuition: ``across $\partial\Omega$'' means the operator confronts the
admissibility boundary (tight constraints) rather than moving freely inside a
smooth interior.

\subsection{Outcome evaluation}

We evaluate interventions by two structural observables:
\[
\Delta k(\omega) := k(\mathcal{I}(\omega)) - k(\omega),
\qquad
\Delta \Phase(\omega) := \Phase(\mathcal{I}(\omega)) - \Phase(\omega),
\]
where the second is symbolic: it indicates whether a phase transition occurs.
We will only use the predicate ``phase transition happens''.

\begin{definition}[Phase-preserving]
$\mathcal{I}$ is \emph{phase-preserving} on $\Dom(\mathcal{I})$ if
\[
\Phase(\mathcal{I}(\omega))=\Phase(\omega)\quad \forall \omega\in\Dom(\mathcal{I}).
\]
\end{definition}
