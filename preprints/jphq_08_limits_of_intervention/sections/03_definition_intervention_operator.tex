\section{Intervention Operator}

\subsection{Definition}

\begin{definition}[Intervention operator]
An \emph{intervention} is a (possibly partial) operator
\[
\mathcal{I}:\Dom(\mathcal{I})\subseteq \Omega \to \Omega,
\]
interpreted as a state update $\omega \mapsto \mathcal{I}(\omega)$.

The operator $\mathcal{I}$ is \emph{$\Theta$-preserving} if for every
$\omega\in\Dom(\mathcal{I})$,
\[
\ThetaSys(\omega)\le 0
\quad \Rightarrow \quad
\ThetaSys(\mathcal{I}(\omega))\le 0.
\]
\end{definition}

No objective functions, optimality criteria, governance semantics, or control
interpretations are attached to $\mathcal{I}$.
It is treated purely as an abstract state transformation subject to feasibility
constraints.

\subsection{Boundary-crossing action}

\begin{definition}[Acts across the boundary]
An intervention operator $\mathcal{I}$ \emph{acts across} the admissible boundary
$\partial\Omega$ if it cannot be represented as an internal re-parameterization
of $\Omega$ that leaves the boundary invariant.

Formally, $\mathcal{I}$ acts across $\partial\Omega$ if there exists no extension
$\tilde{\mathcal{I}}$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\tilde{\mathcal{I}}$ is an automorphism of $\Omega$, and
\item $\tilde{\mathcal{I}}(\partial\Omega)=\partial\Omega$, and
\item $\mathcal{I}$ is the restriction of $\tilde{\mathcal{I}}$ to
$\Dom(\mathcal{I})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Intuitively, acting across $\partial\Omega$ means that the operator confronts
constraint-tight regions where admissibility is enforced by thresholds, rather
than moving freely within an unconstrained interior.

\subsection{Outcome observables}

We evaluate interventions using two structural observables.

First, the change in continuity:
\[
\Delta k(\omega) := k(\mathcal{I}(\omega)) - k(\omega).
\]

Second, a qualitative phase-change predicate indicating whether a phase
transition occurs:
\[
\text{PhaseChange}(\omega) :=
\bigl[\Phase(\mathcal{I}(\omega)) \neq \Phase(\omega)\bigr].
\]

No ordering, metric, or algebraic structure on phase labels is assumed.

\begin{definition}[Phase-preserving]
An intervention $\mathcal{I}$ is \emph{phase-preserving} on $\Dom(\mathcal{I})$ if
\[
\Phase(\mathcal{I}(\omega))=\Phase(\omega)
\quad \forall \omega\in\Dom(\mathcal{I}).
\]
\end{definition}
