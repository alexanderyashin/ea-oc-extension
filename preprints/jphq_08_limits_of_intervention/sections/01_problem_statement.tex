\section{Problem Statement}

We model the enterprise as a continuum $K_{EA}$ with admissible state space
$\Omega(K_{EA})$ governed by a non-negotiable threshold system $\Theta$.
An \emph{intervention} is any operator that maps admissible states to admissible
states subject to these thresholds.

The central question of this work is intentionally negative:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Where is intervention structurally impossible, self-defeating, or
destructive, independent of intent, competence, or declared objectives?}
\end{quote}

This question is typically not addressed by intervention, transformation, or
change-management frameworks, which implicitly assume feasibility and instead
focus on optimization, governance quality, or execution capability.
Such approaches presuppose that admissible transformations exist and ask only
how well they can be carried out.

In contrast, we ask whether feasibility itself can fail for \emph{structural}
reasons.
Specifically, we ask whether there exist configurations of the enterprise
continuum for which \emph{no} intervention operator can simultaneously
(i) respect all admissibility thresholds, (ii) avoid loss of structural
continuity, and (iii) preserve the qualitative phase of the system.

Accordingly, this work does not study optimal, effective, or well-governed
intervention.
Instead, it seeks to characterize \emph{structural limits} implied solely by the
existence of admissibility boundaries $\partial\Omega$ and inequality-based
threshold constraints $\Theta$.
These limits are independent of managerial intent, organizational maturity, or
implementation skill and persist even under idealized intervention capability.

The problem addressed is therefore not one of performance or control, but of
\emph{existence}.
We ask whether there are general conditions under which any intervention that
confronts the admissibility boundary is structurally forced into a fundamental
tradeoff: either a degradation of continuity within the admissible state space
or a qualitative transition of system phase.
