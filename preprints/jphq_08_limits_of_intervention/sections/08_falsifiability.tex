\section{Falsifiability}

The results of this work are falsifiable in the strict Popperian sense.
They are refuted if and only if one can \emph{explicitly construct} an
intervention operator $\mathcal{I}$ together with a nontrivial domain
$D\subseteq\Omega$ such that \emph{all} of the following conditions hold
simultaneously.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Boundary action.}
$\mathcal{I}$ acts across $\partial\Omega$ (Definition in Section~3), i.e., it
genuinely confronts constraint-tight regions rather than remaining an interior
re-parameterization of $\Omega$.

\item \textbf{Threshold preservation.}
$\mathcal{I}$ is $\ThetaSys$-preserving on $D$:
\[
\ThetaSys(\omega)\le 0 \;\Rightarrow\;
\ThetaSys(\mathcal{I}(\omega))\le 0
\quad \forall \omega\in D.
\]

\item \textbf{No continuity loss.}
$\mathcal{I}$ does not reduce structural continuity on $D$:
\[
k(\mathcal{I}(\omega)) \ge k(\omega)
\quad \forall \omega\in D.
\]

\item \textbf{No phase transition.}
$\mathcal{I}$ is phase-preserving on $D$:
\[
\Phase(\mathcal{I}(\omega))=\Phase(\omega)
\quad \forall \omega\in D.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Failure of any single condition does \emph{not} constitute a refutation.
Only the simultaneous satisfaction of (1)--(4) on a non-negligible domain $D$
invalidates Theorem~\ref{thm:no_free} and thereby refutes the structural limits
derived in this work.

\paragraph{Operational refutation test (ETS-consistent).}
A concrete refutation path is to implement $\mathcal{I}$ in an executable
environment consistent with ETS.K\_EA.v1.1 and demonstrate that, for a family of
states sampling neighborhoods that include constraint-tight regions, properties
(1)--(4) hold simultaneously on a domain $D$ of non-negligible measure.
Any such explicit construction constitutes a direct operational refutation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:no_free}.
