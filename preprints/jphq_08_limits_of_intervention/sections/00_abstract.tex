\begin{abstract}
We investigate the formal limits of intervention in enterprises modeled as
structural continua under the Ontology of Continua (OC Core) framework and an
executable-closed enterprise specification (ETS.K\_EA.v1.1).
The focus is deliberately negative and structural: we ask under which conditions
intervention is provably impossible, self-defeating, or phase-destructive,
independent of intent, competence, or governance quality.

Intervention is formalized as an abstract operator acting on the admissible state
space $\Omega(K_{EA})$ subject to a non-negotiable threshold system $\Theta$.
Feasibility is characterized as invariance under $\Theta$, boundary action as
confrontation with the admissibility boundary $\partial\Omega$, and outcomes via
a coarse phase partition
$\{\OK,\INERTIA,\COLLAPSE,\STOP\}$.

The main results establish \emph{no-free} limits:
any intervention that genuinely acts across $\partial\Omega$ while preserving
all thresholds must either degrade structural continuity $k$ or induce a phase
transition.
The framework is explicitly falsifiable: it is refuted if one can construct an
intervention operator that crosses $\partial\Omega$, preserves $\Theta$, maintains
or increases $k$, and avoids any phase transition on a non-negligible domain.
\end{abstract}
