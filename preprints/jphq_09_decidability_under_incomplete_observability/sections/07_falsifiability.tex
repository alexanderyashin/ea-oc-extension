\section{Falsifiability}
\label{sec:falsifiability}

All results in this paper are formulated so as to admit explicit and
unambiguous falsification \emph{within} the Ontology of Continua (OC
Core) and the executable specification ETS.K\_EA.v1.1. Falsifiability is
defined strictly at the level of admissible observability constructs
and logical invariance properties. No external semantics, empirical
methods, or auxiliary assumptions are invoked or required.

\subsection{Falsifiability of decidability claims}
\label{subsec:falsifiability-decidable}

A decidability claim for a state predicate $\varphi$ asserts that
$\varphi$ is invariant across all enterprise continua that are
observationally equivalent under the ETS-defined observation operator
$\Pi$.

Such a claim is falsified if and only if there exist:
\begin{itemize}
\item two enterprise continua $K_{EA}^{(1)}$ and $K_{EA}^{(2)}$ that are
      observationally equivalent, i.e.\
      \[
      \Pi(\Omega_{EA}^{(1)}) = \Pi(\Omega_{EA}^{(2)}),
      \]
\item an admissible state predicate $\varphi$ such that
      \[
      \varphi(\Omega_{EA}^{(1)}) \neq \varphi(\Omega_{EA}^{(2)}).
      \]
\end{itemize}

The existence of such a pair constitutes a direct logical
counterexample and invalidates the decidability claim. No probabilistic,
algorithmic, asymptotic, or methodological considerations enter this
criterion. A single counterexample suffices.

\subsection{Falsifiability of undecidability claims}
\label{subsec:falsifiability-undecidable}

An undecidability claim asserts the \emph{existence} of at least one
observational equivalence class on which a predicate $\varphi$ is
non-invariant.

Such a claim is falsified if and only if it can be shown that
$\varphi$ is invariant across \emph{all} observational equivalence
classes. Concretely, falsification occurs if at least one of the
following holds:
\begin{itemize}
\item for every pair of observationally equivalent enterprise continua,
      the predicate $\varphi$ agrees;
\item there exists a mapping
      \[
      \tilde{\varphi} : \Sigma_{\mathrm{obs}} \to
      \{\text{true},\text{false}\}
      \]
      such that $\varphi = \tilde{\varphi} \circ \Pi$.
\end{itemize}

Either condition establishes global invariance of $\varphi$ under
observational equivalence and therefore contradicts the undecidability
claim.

\subsection{Falsifiability of STOP-only claims}
\label{subsec:falsifiability-stop-only}

STOP-only decidability claims assert a one-sided invariance structure:
STOP membership is invariant under observational equivalence, while
non-STOP outcomes are not.

Such a claim is falsified if and only if at least one of the following
conditions holds:
\begin{itemize}
\item there exist observationally equivalent enterprise continua for
      which STOP holds in one case but not in the other;
\item there exists at least one non-STOP phase outcome that is invariant
      across all observationally equivalent continua.
\end{itemize}

The first case violates STOP invariance directly. The second collapses
the STOP-only classification into full decidability, contradicting the
original claim.

\subsection{Observable-signature-level refutation}
\label{subsec:falsifiability-signatures}

All falsification conditions reduce to statements about observable
signatures in $\Sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and their compatibility with
OC/ETS-admissible predicates. No falsification requires access to
unobservable internal structure beyond what is already constrained by
ETS observability.

In particular, the existence of a single admissible observable
signature $\sigma \in \Sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}$ that is compatible with
mutually inconsistent predicate values is sufficient to refute any
claim of decidability for the corresponding predicate.

\subsection{No meta-level escape}
\label{subsec:no-meta-escape}

No result in this paper can be falsified or defended by appeal to
improved measurement, richer data streams, probabilistic modeling, or
algorithmic inference \emph{within the same OC/ETS framework}. Any such
appeal would constitute a change of formal system and therefore lies
outside the scope of admissible falsification for the present results.

Within OC Core and ETS.K\_EA.v1.1, falsifiability is complete and
exhaustive at the logical level specified above.
