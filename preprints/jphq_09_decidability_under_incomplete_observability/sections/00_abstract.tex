\begin{abstract}
This paper addresses a single, fixed question: which enterprise state
predicates are logically decidable, and which are provably undecidable,
under incomplete observability when enterprises are modeled as
continua $K_{EA}$ within the Ontology of Continua (OC Core) and its
executable specification ETS.K\_EA.v1.1.

Decidability is treated strictly as a logical property of predicates
under observational equivalence. It is explicitly separated from
algorithmic decidability, statistical inference, estimation,
reconstruction, or procedural decision-making. Observability is
formalized solely through the ETS-defined observation operator and the
associated space of observable signatures, without assuming injectivity,
invertibility, convergence, or completeness.

We show that a state predicate is decidable under incomplete
observability if and only if it is invariant across all observational
equivalence classes induced by admissible observations. Predicates that
depend on internal structural features not uniquely fixed by observable
signatures are undecidable by construction. As a central negative
result, we prove that most non-terminal phase distinctions cannot be
decided under incomplete observability.

A sharp boundary case is identified in the form of \emph{STOP-only
decidability}: under explicit OC/ETS conditions, STOP membership is
observationally invariant and therefore decidable, even though all
non-STOP phase distinctions remain undecidable. This yields a complete
trichotomy of admissible enterprise state predicates into decidable,
undecidable, and STOP-only decidable classes.

All results are fully internal to the OC/ETS formalism and are
explicitly falsifiable at the level of observable signatures. The paper
introduces no inference, probabilistic assumptions, governance models,
optimization criteria, or intervention semantics, and establishes
formal limits on what can and cannot be decided about enterprise states
from admissible observations alone.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} enterprise continuum; incomplete observability;
logical decidability; indistinguishability; diagnosability; STOP.
