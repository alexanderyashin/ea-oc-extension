\begin{definition}[Evolution (structural persistence)]
An \emph{evolution} of $\KEA$ is any admissible change that remains entirely
internal to a fixed structural type. Formally, an evolution is a trajectory
whose states all lie in $\Om(\KEA)$ and whose description preserves the
primitive signature
\[
\langle \Om, \dOm, \Ax, \Pot, \Th, \Flow, \Cyc, \kcont \rangle
\]
up to equivalence relations already admitted by \OC. In particular, an
evolution does not require:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the introduction of any axis incompatible with $\Ax(\KEA)$; nor
  \item any modification of thresholds $\Th(\KEA)$ that would render the
  original admissible state set $\Om(\KEA)$ non-viable or empty.
\end{enumerate}
All admissibility conditions remain satisfied throughout the evolution.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Transformation (structural type change)]
A \emph{transformation} of $\KEA$ is a change that cannot be represented as a
trajectory entirely contained in $\Om(\KEA)$ under a fixed admissible primitive
signature. Equivalently, a transformation is a change that requires a change of
structural type and therefore necessarily entails at least one of the following
conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the introduction of an axis not contained in $\Ax(\KEA)$ whose
  constraints are incompatible with the existing axes or thresholds; or
  \item a reconfiguration of thresholds $\Th(\KEA)$ such that the original
  admissible state set $\Om(\KEA)$ becomes non-viable or empty.
\end{enumerate}
In either case, the original continuum structure is insufficient to represent
the resulting states as admissible within the same enterprise continuum
$\KEA$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The distinction between evolution and transformation is purely structural and
not temporal. A change occurring arbitrarily slowly may still constitute a
transformation if it requires a change of structural type, while a rapid,
discontinuous, or externally induced change may qualify as an evolution
provided it remains representable entirely within the same admissible
structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
No assumptions are made about agency, intent, effort, intervention, or control.
The classification of a change as evolution or transformation depends solely on
its representability within the admissible structure of the continuum, as
defined by $\Om(\KEA)$, $\Ax(\KEA)$, and $\Th(\KEA)$.
\end{remark}
