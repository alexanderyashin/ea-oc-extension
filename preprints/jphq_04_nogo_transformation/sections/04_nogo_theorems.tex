% JPHQ-04 — No-Go theorems (OC/ETS-internal, diagnostic-only)

This section states formal No-Go theorems for enterprise transformation as
pure impossibility results within the \OC/\kw{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1} framework. Each
theorem identifies a structural obstruction that rules out transformation as
defined in Section~3. No result asserts inevitability of collapse, success, or
failure; each asserts only the non-existence of \emph{admissible}
transformations under explicitly stated OC/ETS constraints.

\begin{theorem}[Axis-Incompatibility Barrier]
\label{thm:axis-incompatibility}
Let $\KEA$ be an enterprise continuum with axis set $\Ax(\KEA)$, thresholds
$\Th(\KEA)$, and admissible state set $\Om(\KEA)$. Assume that for any candidate
transformation of $\KEA$ there exists an axis
$A_{\mathrm{new}} \notin \Ax(\KEA)$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the transformed structural type requires inclusion of
  $A_{\mathrm{new}}$; and
  \item the constraint set induced by $\Ax(\KEA)\cup\{A_{\mathrm{new}}\}$ is
  incompatible with $\Th(\KEA)$ in the sense that no state satisfies the
  resulting axis-and-threshold constraints.
\end{enumerate}
Then no admissible transformation of $\KEA$ exists. Any realization of the
postulated transformed structural type yields
$\Om(\KEA)=\varnothing$ under the attempted extension and is therefore classified
by the \OC/\kw{ETS} phase logic as $\PHCO$ or $\PHST$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Threshold Viability Barrier]
\label{thm:threshold-barrier}
Let $\KEA$ be an enterprise continuum with axis set $\Ax(\KEA)$ and thresholds
$\Th(\KEA)$. Assume that any transformation of $\KEA$ requires a threshold
reconfiguration $\Th'(\KEA)$ such that the corresponding admissible state set is
empty:
\[
\Om(\KEA;\Th')=\varnothing .
\]
Then transformation of $\KEA$ is impossible within \OC/\kw{ETS}. Under any such
required threshold configuration, no admissible states exist, and hence no
admissible trajectory or structural type change can be represented.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Cycle-Closure Obstruction]
\label{thm:cycle-closure}
Let $\KEA$ be an enterprise continuum with admissible cycle set $\Cyc(\KEA)$ and
continuity measure $\kcont(\KEA)$. Assume that every candidate transformed
structural type entails a configuration for which no admissible cycle remains,
i.e.,
\[
\Cyc(\KEA)=\varnothing
\quad\text{(in the postulated transformed structure).}
\]
If, in addition, the OC/ETS continuity condition requires existence of at least
one admissible cycle for $\kcont(\KEA)>0$, then $\kcont(\KEA)=0$ under the
postulated transformation and the continuum is classified as $\PHST$.
Consequently, transformation is structurally impossible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Phase-Incompatibility Theorem]
\label{thm:phase-incompatibility}
Assume that the \OC/\kw{ETS} phase-transition constraints for $\KEA$ forbid any
admissible phase-transition path from the current phase of $\KEA$ to a phase
compatible with a transformed structural type. Then no admissible
transformation of $\KEA$ exists. In particular, if all admissible paths in the
phase-transition graph from the initial phase to any phase supporting the
postulated transformed type are disallowed, transformation is impossible within
the OC/ETS phase logic, independently of temporal duration, effort, agency, or
context.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Boundary Obstruction Theorem]
\label{thm:boundary-obstruction}
Let $\dOm(\KEA)$ denote the boundary of the admissible state set $\Om(\KEA)$.
Assume that any trajectory required for transformation must cross $\dOm(\KEA)$
in a manner not admitted by \OC/\kw{ETS} boundary conditions, i.e., the required
crossing violates the OC/ETS admissibility rules governing boundary interaction.
Then transformation of $\KEA$ is impossible: the boundary constitutes a
structural obstruction that prevents any admissible access to the region of
state space required by the transformed structural type.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Existence of Structurally Non-Transformable Classes]
There exist non-empty classes of enterprise continua $\KEA$ for which all
candidate transformations are ruled out by at least one of
Theorems~\ref{thm:axis-incompatibility}--\ref{thm:boundary-obstruction}. For such
classes, transformation is formally impossible within \OC/\kw{ETS},
independently of time, intent, agency, or intervention.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The No-Go theorems stated above are logically independent at the level of OC/ETS
assumptions: a given enterprise continuum may satisfy multiple No-Go conditions
simultaneously or exactly one. No claim is made regarding empirical prevalence,
frequency, or desirability of such cases; the results are purely structural.
\end{remark}
