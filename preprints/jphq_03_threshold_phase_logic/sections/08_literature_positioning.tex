\section{Literature positioning (minimal)}
\label{sec:lit}

The present work adopts the notion of a phase boundary from statistical physics
as a \emph{structural analogy} for threshold-induced partitions of admissible
state spaces. No claim of physical identity, shared dynamics, or universality
classes is made. The analogy is limited to the existence of constraint-defined
boundaries across which classification changes discontinuously.

In physics, phase transitions are characterized by non-analytic behavior induced
by control parameters and thresholds \cite{stanley1971phase}, and by qualitative
regime changes in nonlinear systems \cite{strogatz2018nonlinear}. This paper does
not import thermodynamic variables, order parameters, or dynamical laws from that
literature. Instead, it isolates a minimal structural correspondence: Boolean or
threshold predicates that induce discontinuous changes in admissible classifications.

Within socio-technical and organizational research, related notions such as
resilience, regime shifts, or collapse have been discussed descriptively
(e.g., \cite{holling1973resilience}), often with implicit normative or managerial
interpretations. In contrast, the present work remains strictly formal and
diagnostic: phases are defined exclusively as predicate-induced subsets of
$\Omega(K_{EA})$, without prescribing responses or interventions.

The Ontology of Continua (OC Core) and the executable specification ETS.K\_EA.v1.1
are treated as internal formal sources \cite{oc_core_internal,ets_keav11_internal}.
They provide the complete symbol set, predicates, and invariants used in this
paper, and no external theoretical framework is introduced.

The novelty of the contribution lies solely in the explicit formalization of
phase logic in socio-technical enterprises as a threshold-induced partition of an
admissible state space, together with precise falsifiability conditions. No claims
are made beyond this structural correspondence.
