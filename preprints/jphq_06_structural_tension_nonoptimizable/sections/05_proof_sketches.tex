\section{Proof Sketches and Boundary Cases}

This section provides finite proof sketches for the impossibility results
stated above. All arguments are structural and logical. No appeal is made
to empirical data, numerical instantiation, or external optimization
theory. Each sketch isolates the minimal contradiction with OC/ETS
semantics.

\subsection{Proof Sketch for Result 1}

Assume that a scalar function
\[
\phi : \OmegaEA \rightarrow \mathbb{R}
\]
exists and represents structural tension $\TEA$.

By construction, $\phi$ induces an ordering on $\OmegaEA$ via the
standard order on $\mathbb{R}$. In \texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}, the only
admissible relations over states are:
(i) sign-based evaluations of the component functions $f_k$, and
(ii) ordinal phase precedence.

No rule exists to compare two admissible states belonging to the same
phase when all relevant $f_k \leq 0$. Any ordering introduced by $\phi$
therefore exceeds what is derivable from the theory. This violates the
scope lock of OC/ETS. Hence, no such scalar representation exists within
the theory.

\subsection{Proof Sketch for Result 2}

Assume that an order $\preceq$ exists on $\OmegaEA$ interpreted as
``less than or equal structural tension''.

Phase precedence defines dominance relations between disjoint classes of
states (for example, \PHASESTOP\ dominates \PHASECOLLAPSE). Within any
single phase, OC/ETS defines no further discriminants.

Any order $\preceq$ must therefore either:
(i) refine ordering within a phase by introducing new criteria, or
(ii) collapse distinct phases into a single ordered scale.

Both options introduce structure not present in OC/ETS and alter the
formal object under consideration. Therefore, no admissible optimization
order exists.

\subsection{Proof Sketch for Result 3}

Assume that structural tension $\TEA$ is treated as an objective for
minimization or maximization over $\OmegaEA$.

Optimization semantics require a notion of improvement defined over
admissible states. In OC/ETS, admissibility is a logical predicate, not a
gradable or optimizable property. Thresholds delimit where diagnosis is
defined; they are not extrema of a quantity.

Reinterpreting thresholds as optimization targets reassigns them from
constraints of existence to objectives of improvement. This changes their
formal role and violates admissibility semantics. Hence, optimization of
$\TEA$ is incompatible with admissibility preservation.

\subsection{Proof Sketch for Result 4}

Assume that a monotonic mapping
\[
g : \TEA \rightarrow \kEA
\]
exists.

Continuity $\kEA$ is defined through cycle closure, observability, and
parent compatibility. Structural tension is defined through threshold
inequalities. These predicates are logically independent in OC/ETS.

A monotonic mapping would permit substitution of $\kEA$ for $\TEA$ as an
optimization proxy. Such substitution constitutes a change of formal
objects and violates the \emph{Metric Substitution} no-go constraint.
Therefore, no such mapping exists.

\subsection{Proof Sketch for Result 5}

Assume that extremization is applied to $\TEA$.

Extremization presupposes an ordered scale. Applying it to $\TEA$ treats
phases as ordered levels. Phase logic, however, is ordinal and
precedence-based: \PHASESTOP\ dominates \PHASECOLLAPSE\ by rule, not by
magnitude.

Interpreting phases as extrema erases this logical asymmetry and induces
inadmissible transitions between diagnostic classes. Thus, extremization
collapses phase semantics.

\subsection{Boundary Cases}

At the boundary $\dOmegaEA$, the only admissible comparisons are those
explicitly encoded in phase precedence. No limit process approaching
$\dOmegaEA$ defines an interior distance, gradient, margin, or rate of
change.

Consequently, epsilon-margin reasoning, asymptotic optimization, or
``safety buffer'' interpretations are excluded by construction. Such
arguments presuppose metric structure that is absent from OC/ETS.
