\section*{Abstract}

This paper examines the problem of \emph{structural diagnosability} of enterprise states under conditions of incomplete and indirect observability.
Although enterprises are commonly described through metrics, indicators, and outcome variables, such observables do not uniquely determine the internal structural configurations that generate them.
Consequently, distinct enterprise structures may give rise to indistinguishable observable projections, imposing fundamental limits on diagnostic inference.

Within the formal framework of the Enterprise Continuum \(K_{EA}\) and its executable specification ETS.K\_EA.v1.1, we analyze diagnosability as an epistemic property of the mapping from internal structural spaces to observable domains.
The analysis is strictly diagnostic and non-prescriptive: it does not address control, optimization, intervention, or corrective action.
Its focus is instead on the conditions under which an enterprise state can be inferred to belong to a \emph{structural phase class}, rather than identified as a unique configuration.

The paper makes three contributions.
First, it provides a formal definition of structural diagnosability in terms of distinguishability between equivalence classes induced by observable projections.
Second, it introduces a phase-based interpretation of diagnostic claims, demonstrating that, in general, well-founded diagnoses are phase-level rather than state-level assertions.
Third, it specifies falsifiability conditions for diagnostic statements by characterizing observable distinctions whose presence would refute phase membership.

The scope of the results is intentionally limited.
No claims are made regarding desirable states, optimal structures, or recommended actions.
Non-diagnosability is treated as a valid and informative outcome, reflecting intrinsic epistemic constraints rather than model inadequacy.
Under these conditions, structural diagnosability is established as a formally bounded property of enterprise models, determined jointly by observability and structural organization.
