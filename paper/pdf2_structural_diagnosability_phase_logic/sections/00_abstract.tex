\section*{Abstract}

This paper addresses the problem of \emph{structural diagnosability} of enterprise states under incomplete and indirect observability.
While enterprises are routinely described through metrics, indicators, and outcome variables, such observable signals do not uniquely determine the internal structural configuration that generates them.
As a result, distinct enterprise states may exhibit indistinguishable observable manifestations, limiting the validity of diagnostic inference.

Within the formal framework of the Enterprise Continuum \(K_{EA}\) and its executable specification ETS.K\_EA.v1.1, we investigate the conditions under which an enterprise state is diagnosable as a member of a structural phase class, rather than as a unique configuration.
The analysis is explicitly non-prescriptive and does not address control, optimization, or intervention.
Instead, it focuses on the epistemic boundaries of what can and cannot be inferred from observable projections of enterprise structure.

The paper makes three contributions.
First, it provides a formal definition of structural diagnosability as a property of the mapping between internal structural spaces and observable projections.
Second, it introduces a phase-based interpretation of diagnosability, showing that diagnosable statements are, in general, phase-level rather than state-level claims.
Third, it characterizes falsifiability conditions for diagnostic statements by identifying observable distinctions that would refute phase membership.

The scope of the paper is intentionally restricted.
No claims are made about desirable states, optimal configurations, or corrective actions.
Failure of diagnosability is treated as a legitimate and informative outcome, not as a deficiency to be remedied.
The results establish structural diagnosability as an epistemic property of enterprise models, subject to formal limits imposed by observability and model structure.
