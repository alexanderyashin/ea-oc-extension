\section{Formal Setting}

This section establishes the formal and epistemic context required for the diagnostic analysis developed in this paper.
Rather than introducing a complete mathematical model of the enterprise, the formal setting clarifies the logical status of structural claims under conditions of incomplete observability.
Its purpose is to delineate the boundaries within which diagnostic statements are meaningful, and to make explicit the assumptions under which such statements can be evaluated as valid, invalid, or underdetermined.

The enterprise is treated as a structured system whose internal configuration is not directly accessible, but only observable through partial and indirect projections.
Accordingly, the formal setting does not aim at full state reconstruction or identifiability.
Instead, it provides the minimal conceptual and formal apparatus necessary to reason about distinguishability, equivalence, and phase membership in the presence of structural non-identifiability.

As part of this clarification, the present work is explicitly positioned with respect to adjacent literatures.
The purpose of this positioning is not to survey methods, compare approaches, or assess empirical performance.
It is to identify which commonly adopted assumptions—such as full observability, state identifiability, or controllability—do not hold in a diagnostic-first regime, and why the failure of identifiability must be treated as a first-class and informative outcome rather than as a deficiency to be eliminated.
