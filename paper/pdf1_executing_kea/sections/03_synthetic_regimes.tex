\section{Synthetic Regimes}
\label{sec:synthetic-regimes}

\subsection{Purpose of synthetic regimes}
Synthetic regimes are used here as \emph{diagnostic abstractions} for grouping
regions of the enterprise phase space that exhibit structurally similar behavior
under observation.

They are not empirical clusters, forecasts, or managerial categories.
Their sole purpose is to provide a stable descriptive layer for reasoning about
diagnostic outcomes already implied by the KEA framework.

\subsection{Regime definition (container)}
A synthetic regime denotes a subset of the admissible phase space characterized by:
\begin{itemize}
  \item shared structural constraints,
  \item comparable boundary proximity with respect to STOP regions,
  \item invariant qualitative behavior under admissible diagnostic projections.
\end{itemize}

All regime boundaries and descriptors are inherited from existing KEA/ETS definitions.
No new regime taxonomy is introduced in this document.

\subsection{Relation to phase space}
Each synthetic regime occupies a well-defined region (possibly non-convex or
disconnected) within the constructed phase space.
The mapping between regimes and phase space regions is many-to-many and purely
descriptive.

This section intentionally avoids any ranking, scoring, or prioritization of regimes.

\subsection{Stability and transitions (non-prescriptive)}
Observed transitions between regimes are treated as \emph{diagnostic signals}.
They do not imply controllability, desirability, or reversibility.

No transition rules, probabilities, or causal mechanisms are specified here.

\subsection{Explicit exclusions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item No lifecycle or maturity models.
  \item No success/failure labeling.
  \item No optimization paths between regimes.
  \item No implied intervention recommendations.
\end{itemize}
