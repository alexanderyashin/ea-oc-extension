\section{Executing Diagnostics}
\label{sec:executing-diagnostics}

\subsection{Role of execution in a diagnostic context}
Execution in this document refers exclusively to the \emph{procedural application}
of already defined diagnostic rules to observed enterprise states.
It does not imply intervention, control, optimization, or decision enforcement.

The term “executing” is used in a strictly technical sense: applying an immutable
specification to concrete observations in a reproducible manner.

\subsection{Diagnostic inputs (container)}
Diagnostic execution assumes the availability of:
\begin{itemize}
  \item observed enterprise signals admissible under the KEA scope,
  \item a fixed mapping into the diagnostic phase space,
  \item invariant boundary and STOP conditions as defined in the ETS.
\end{itemize}

No assumptions are made about data completeness, quality, or timeliness.

\subsection{Execution flow (structural slot)}
This subsection reserves the structural slot for a future step-by-step
diagnostic flow, such as:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item projection of observations into phase space,
  \item identification of the occupied synthetic regime(s),
  \item evaluation of proximity to STOP regions,
  \item generation of diagnostic outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

In RUN-EDHQ-01, this flow is intentionally non-operational and non-algorithmic.

\subsection{Determinism and reproducibility}
Given identical inputs and an unchanged specification, diagnostic execution
must be deterministic and reproducible.
Any future executable realization must preserve this property.

\subsection{Non-claims}
\begin{itemize}
  \item No guarantee of actionability.
  \item No claim of predictive power.
  \item No promise of real-time execution.
  \item No implication of managerial decision support.
\end{itemize}
