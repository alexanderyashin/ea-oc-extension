\section{Thresholds, Components, and Structural Tension}

\subsection{Role of Thresholds in the Continuum}

Thresholds $\Theta_{\mathrm{EA}}$ define admissibility limits for the
enterprise continuum. They separate regions of the state space that are
structurally permissible from those that are not.

Thresholds are not targets, goals, or performance benchmarks. They are
constraints that determine whether the continuum can exist, persist,
and remain diagnosable.

\subsection{Structural Tension and Inequality-Based Formulation}

Structural tension arises when internal dynamics push the enterprise
state toward threshold boundaries. In the formalism of
\texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}, this tension is expressed through component
functions $f_k$ evaluated as inequalities.

No numeric values are fixed at the theory level. The model operates on
the sign and ordering of these components, not on calibrated magnitudes.

\subsection{Threshold Components}

The ETS defines the following threshold components:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $f_{\text{exist}}$: existence admissibility,
  \item $f_{\text{stab}}$: stability and persistence,
  \item $f_{\text{inertia}}$: structural inertia,
  \item $f_{\text{collapse}}$: irreversible structural breakdown,
  \item $f_{\text{stop}}$: termination of diagnosis.
\end{itemize}

Each component corresponds to a subset of $\Theta_{\mathrm{EA}}$ and is
evaluated independently, subject to precedence rules defined in the
phase logic.

\subsection{Interpretation of Components}

\paragraph{Existence Component ($f_{\text{exist}}$).}
This component evaluates whether $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ remains
non-empty. A positive value indicates violation of existence conditions.

\paragraph{Stability Component ($f_{\text{stab}}$).}
This component captures the integrity of required cycles over the
internal time scale $\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

\paragraph{Inertia Component ($f_{\text{inertia}}$).}
This component reflects the inability of the continuum to transition
between admissible states without external intervention.

\paragraph{Collapse Component ($f_{\text{collapse}}$).}
This component signals irreversible loss of structural coherence,
typically following sustained violation of stability conditions.

\paragraph{Termination Component ($f_{\text{stop}}$).}
This component encodes conditions under which diagnosis must terminate,
independent of other indicators.

\subsection{No Implicit Optimization}

The presence of thresholds does not imply that the enterprise should be
kept away from them or steered toward any particular region of the state
space.

Thresholds merely define the boundary of admissible diagnosis. Crossing
certain thresholds invalidates further analysis within the model.

\subsection{Structural Tension Versus Performance Stress}

Structural tension is distinct from operational or financial stress. A
system may experience significant stress while remaining structurally
admissible.

Conversely, a system may violate structural thresholds without obvious
short-term performance degradation. The threshold formalism captures
this distinction explicitly.
