\section{Invariants and No-Go Results}

\subsection{Role of Invariants}

Invariants in $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ express structural properties that hold
across all admissible states of the enterprise continuum.
They are not assumptions, heuristics, or design guidelines.
They follow necessarily from the formal specification given by
\texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}.

Violation of an invariant does not indicate inefficiency or poor
performance.
It indicates that the object under consideration no longer satisfies
the conditions required to be defined as $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

\subsection{Energy of Transformation}

The invariant \emph{Energy of Transformation} states that any transition
between admissible enterprise states requires non-zero structural
expenditure.

There are no cost-free transitions in $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$.
Any interpretation in which large-scale structural change occurs without
corresponding impact on thresholds, cycles, or continuity violates the
formal conditions of admissibility.

Apparent instantaneous or frictionless transformation therefore signals
loss of definability rather than exceptional efficiency.

\subsection{Parent Compatibility}

The invariant \emph{Parent Compatibility} requires that all admissible
enterprise states remain compatible with constraints imposed by the
parent continuum $K_7$.

An enterprise state that violates parent compatibility is not a degraded
or extreme state of $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$.
It lies outside the admissible state space $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ and is
therefore not defined within the continuum.

\subsection{No-Go Results}

No-Go results are formal impossibility statements derived from the
Executable Theory Specification.
They specify transitions, interpretations, or substitutions that are
structurally forbidden within $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

These results delimit the domain in which diagnostic statements remain
meaningful.

\subsection{Metric Substitution}

The No-Go result \emph{Metric Substitution} forbids replacing structural
diagnosis with isolated scalar performance metrics.

No finite set of metrics can substitute for evaluation of admissibility
conditions, recurrent cycles, and continuity.
Any attempt to do so collapses the diagnostic object by severing its
connection to the defining primitives of the continuum.

\subsection{Instant Recovery}

The No-Go result \emph{Instant Recovery} forbids interpretations in which
an enterprise exits the COLLAPSE or STOP phase without traversing
admissible transitions.

Recovery, if defined at all, requires reconstitution of an admissible
state space and re-establishment of required cycles.
Direct re-entry into SUCCESS without such reconstitution is undefined
within the model.

\subsection{No-Go Results as Integrity Constraints}

No-Go results do not weaken the model or restrict its applicability.
They function as integrity constraints that preserve executable closure
and ontological consistency.

By excluding structurally inconsistent interpretations, they ensure
that diagnostic statements derived from $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ remain
well-defined and reviewable.
