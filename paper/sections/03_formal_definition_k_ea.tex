\section{Formal Definition of the Enterprise Continuum}

\subsection{Definition of $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$}

The Enterprise Continuum $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ is defined as an
Ontology-of-Continua (OC) continuum instantiated according to the
immutable Executable Theory Specification
\texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}.

Formally, the continuum is given by the tuple:
\[
K_{\mathrm{EA}} :=
\left(
\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}},
\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}},
A_{\mathrm{EA}},
P_{\mathrm{EA}},
\Theta_{\mathrm{EA}},
J_{\mathrm{EA}},
C_{\mathrm{EA}},
k_{\mathrm{EA}},
\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}
\right).
\]

Each component is defined below strictly by projection from the ETS.
No additional structure is introduced.

\subsection{State Space and Boundary}

The admissible state space $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ is the set of all
enterprise states that satisfy the structural constraints encoded in
$\Theta_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

The boundary $\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ delimits admissible from
non-admissible states. Crossing this boundary results in either a phase
transition or termination of the continuum.

The non-emptiness of $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ is a necessary condition for
the existence of $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ as a diagnosable object.

\subsection{Axes and Degrees of Freedom}

The set of axes $A_{\mathrm{EA}}$ defines the admissible degrees of
freedom along which enterprise states may vary. Axes are grouped into
structural, governance, operational, resource, informational, and risk
dimensions.

Axes are descriptive, not prescriptive. They do not encode desired
values or optimization targets. They serve solely to parameterize
admissible variation within $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

\subsection{Potentials and Flows}

Potentials $P_{\mathrm{EA}}$ represent stored structural capacities
within the enterprise continuum. Flows $J_{\mathrm{EA}}$ describe the
movement of the system through its state space under internal dynamics
and external constraints.

No assumption of equilibrium, efficiency, or optimality is made.
Potentials and flows are evaluated only insofar as they affect
admissibility with respect to thresholds.

\subsection{Thresholds and Admissibility}

The threshold set $\Theta_{\mathrm{EA}}$ encodes admissibility
conditions via component functions $f_k$ as specified in the ETS.

Thresholds define limits beyond which:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the continuum changes phase,
  \item structural integrity degrades irreversibly,
  \item or diagnosis must terminate.
\end{itemize}

Thresholds are expressed as inequalities. No numeric values are fixed at
the theory level.

\subsection{Cycles and Persistence}

The set of cycles $C_{\mathrm{EA}}$ defines the minimal recurrent
processes required for persistence over the internal time scale
$\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

Cycles are structural necessities. Their absence or breakdown indicates
loss of persistence, even if instantaneous admissibility conditions are
momentarily satisfied.

\subsection{Continuity Measure}

The continuity measure $k_{\mathrm{EA}} \in [0,1]$ quantifies the degree
of structural connectivity of the continuum.

A value of $k_{\mathrm{EA}} = 0$ represents a hard termination condition.
Intermediate values indicate partial connectivity under the admissible
dynamics.

\subsection{Internal Time Scale}

The parameter $\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$ represents the characteristic time
scale over which cycles must close for the continuum to be considered
persistent.

No assumption is made regarding the correspondence of $\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$
to calendar time. It is an internal structural parameter.

\subsection{Executable Closure}

All components of $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ are fully specified within
\texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}. The definition is therefore executable-closed:
no additional assumptions or external parameters are required to define
the continuum.

This closure is a necessary condition for reproducibility and formal
review.
