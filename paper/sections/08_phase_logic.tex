\section{Phase Logic}

\subsection{Purpose of Phase Classification}

Phase logic in $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ provides a coarse-grained classification
of enterprise states with respect to admissible diagnosis.
Phases are not stages of development, maturity levels, or indicators of
progress.
They are labels assigned solely on the basis of threshold evaluations
and continuity conditions.

The purpose of phase logic is to determine which classes of diagnostic
statements remain formally admissible under the current structural
conditions of the enterprise continuum.

\subsection{Precedence Rules}

Phase assignment follows a strict precedence order defined in
\texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}:
\[
\text{STOP} \;\succ\; \text{COLLAPSE} \;\succ\; \text{INERTIA} \;\succ\;
\text{SUCCESS}.
\]

Higher-precedence phases override lower-precedence classifications.
This ordering prevents contradictory diagnoses by ensuring that
violations of existence, termination, or stability conditions dominate
all other considerations.

\subsection{STOP Phase}

The STOP phase represents termination of admissible diagnosis.
It is entered when any of the following conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the existence component is violated ($f_{\text{exist}} > 0$),
  \item termination conditions are met ($f_{\text{stop}} > 0$),
  \item observability required to evaluate admissibility conditions is
        lost.
\end{itemize}

In the STOP phase, further diagnosis is undefined within the model.
No statements regarding recovery, improvement, optimization, or
evolution are admissible.

\subsection{COLLAPSE Phase}

The COLLAPSE phase denotes irreversible loss of structural coherence
while diagnosis remains momentarily defined.

Formally, COLLAPSE holds when:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the collapse component is violated ($f_{\text{collapse}} > 0$),
  \item STOP conditions are not satisfied.
\end{itemize}

COLLAPSE does not imply immediate non-existence.
It indicates that the enterprise continuum can no longer satisfy
persistence requirements over the internal time scale
$\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

\subsection{INERTIA Phase}

The INERTIA phase characterizes states in which the enterprise continuum
remains structurally admissible but lacks endogenous capacity to
transition between admissible states.

Formally, INERTIA holds when:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the inertia component is violated ($f_{\text{inertia}} > 0$),
  \item STOP and COLLAPSE conditions are not satisfied.
\end{itemize}

Diagnosis remains admissible in this phase, but internal dynamics are
structurally constrained.

\subsection{SUCCESS Phase}

The SUCCESS phase is defined by the absence of threshold violations:
\[
f_k \leq 0 \quad \text{for all threshold components } k.
\]

SUCCESS does not imply optimality, efficiency, resilience, or
desirability.
It indicates only that the enterprise continuum satisfies the structural
conditions required for admissible diagnosis.

\subsection{Allowed Transitions}

The Executable Theory Specification defines the following admissible
phase transitions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\text{SUCCESS} \rightarrow \text{INERTIA}, \text{COLLAPSE},
        \text{STOP}$,
  \item $\text{INERTIA} \rightarrow \text{SUCCESS}$ via external
        intervention $\Psi$, or $\text{COLLAPSE}, \text{STOP}$,
  \item $\text{COLLAPSE} \rightarrow \text{STOP}$,
  \item $\text{STOP} \rightarrow$ (none).
\end{itemize}

All transitions are constrained by threshold evaluations and
compatibility with parent continua.
No transition is guaranteed or implied.

\subsection{Non-Teleological Interpretation}

Phase logic encodes no preferred direction of movement.
There is no assumption that SUCCESS should be achieved, maintained, or
restored.

Phases function solely as diagnostic labels delimiting what can and
cannot be meaningfully asserted within the enterprise continuum.
