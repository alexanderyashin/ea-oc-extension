\section{Ontological Grounding: Ontology of Continua Context}

\subsection{Why an Ontological Grounding Is Required}

The Enterprise Continuum $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ is not defined as an isolated
model. It is an instantiation of a general ontological framework known
as the Ontology of Continua (OC). Without an explicit grounding in this
framework, the formal structure of $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ risks being
misinterpreted as a metaphor, a framework, or a descriptive taxonomy.

This section provides the minimal ontological context required to
interpret $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ correctly. It does not restate or modify OC
Core, nor does it introduce new axioms. Its role is strictly
interpretative and delimiting.

\subsection{Continuum as a Primitive Object}

In OC, a continuum is treated as a primitive object characterized not
by its material composition, but by its structural admissibility
conditions. A continuum exists only insofar as its defining primitives
remain well-defined and mutually compatible.

At minimum, a continuum is characterized by:
\begin{itemize}
  \item an admissible state space $\Omega$,
  \item a boundary $\partial\Omega$ delimiting that space,
  \item one or more axes $A$ representing distinguishable degrees of
        freedom,
  \item structural constraints expressed via thresholds $\Theta$.
\end{itemize}

Existence is therefore conditional, not assumed.

\subsection{Dynamics, Cycles, and Persistence}

OC distinguishes between static admissibility and dynamic persistence.
A continuum may satisfy instantaneous admissibility conditions while
failing to persist over time.

Persistence requires the existence of recurrent cycles $C$ that close
over an internal time scale $\tau$. These cycles are not trajectories of
optimization; they are minimal structural loops that prevent the system
from degenerating into a boundary state.

In the absence of such cycles, the continuum may transiently exist but
cannot be diagnosed as a stable object.

\subsection{Continuity and Structural Connectivity}

Continuity in OC is not a geometric notion but a measure of structural
connectivity. The continuity measure $k \in [0,1]$ expresses the degree
to which admissible states remain connected under the defined dynamics.

A value of $k = 0$ indicates a hard structural break: either the state
space is empty or connectivity has collapsed. In such cases, the
continuum ceases to be a valid object of diagnosis.

\subsection{Thresholds and Structural Tension}

Thresholds $\Theta$ define admissibility limits along axes, potentials,
and cycles. Structural tension arises when internal dynamics push the
continuum toward or beyond these limits.

In OC, thresholds are not targets or goals. They are constraints that
separate admissible from non-admissible regimes. Crossing certain
thresholds results in phase transitions or termination of the
continuum.

\subsection{Parent Continua and Compatibility}

A continuum does not exist in isolation. Each continuum is embedded
within one or more parent continua that constrain its admissible state
space.

Compatibility with the parent continuum is a necessary condition for
existence. Violation of parent compatibility does not merely degrade
performance; it invalidates the definition of the child continuum
itself.

\subsection{Implications for Enterprise Diagnosis}

Applying OC to enterprise diagnosis implies that:
\begin{itemize}
  \item enterprises are not assumed to exist unconditionally,
  \item diagnosability is conditional on structural admissibility,
  \item termination of diagnosis is a formal outcome, not a failure.
\end{itemize}

$K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ inherits these implications directly. The enterprise
continuum is therefore subject to the same ontological constraints as
any other OC-defined continuum.

This grounding is essential to interpret later sections of this
whitepaper, particularly those concerning phase logic and STOP
conditions.
