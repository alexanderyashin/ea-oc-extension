\section{Axes and Degrees of Freedom}

\subsection{Role of Axes in the Enterprise Continuum}

Axes in $A_{\mathrm{EA}}$ define the admissible degrees of freedom of the
enterprise continuum. They do not represent objectives, performance
targets, or maturity levels. Their sole role is to parameterize
variation within the admissible state space $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

All axes described in this section are projected directly from
\texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}. No additional axes are introduced, and none are
reinterpreted beyond their structural role.

\subsection{Structural Axes}

Structural axes characterize how enterprise components are arranged and
interconnected.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Coupling}: degree of dependency between components.
  \item \textbf{Modularity}: extent to which components can vary
        independently.
  \item \textbf{Centralization}: concentration of control or decision
        authority.
  \item \textbf{Redundancy}: presence of parallel components or pathways.
\end{itemize}

These axes affect the shape and resilience of $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ but
do not imply preferred configurations.

\subsection{Governance Axes}

Governance axes describe the structural properties of decision-making
and rule enforcement.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Decision latency}: delay between signal and binding
        decision.
  \item \textbf{Rule enforceability}: ability to apply declared rules in
        practice.
  \item \textbf{Escalation integrity}: coherence of escalation paths
        under stress.
\end{itemize}

These axes influence admissibility by constraining the dynamics of
flows and cycles.

\subsection{Operational Axes}

Operational axes characterize the execution layer of the enterprise.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Delivery coherence}: consistency between intent and
        delivered outcomes.
  \item \textbf{Incident recovery loop}: ability to close recovery
        cycles.
  \item \textbf{Change lead time}: delay between decision and effective
        operational change.
\end{itemize}

Operational axes primarily affect persistence and stability conditions.

\subsection{Resource Axes}

Resource axes describe constraints related to material and human
resources.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Budget flexibility}: ability to reallocate financial
        resources.
  \item \textbf{Skills coverage}: availability of required competencies.
  \item \textbf{Supplier dependency}: reliance on external providers.
\end{itemize}

Resource axes constrain potentials $P_{\mathrm{EA}}$ and influence
structural tension.

\subsection{Information Axes}

Information axes characterize observability and semantic coherence.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Observability}: availability of reliable signals about
        system state.
  \item \textbf{Traceability}: ability to reconstruct causal paths.
  \item \textbf{Semantic alignment}: consistency of meaning across
        organizational units.
\end{itemize}

Loss of observability directly affects the admissibility of diagnosis
and may trigger STOP conditions.

\subsection{Risk Axes}

Risk axes describe the visibility and accumulation of unmanaged
structural risk.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Risk visibility}: ability to detect emerging risks.
  \item \textbf{Control gap rate}: rate at which control deficits
        accumulate.
\end{itemize}

These axes influence threshold components related to inertia and
collapse.

\subsection{Axes as Constraints, Not Goals}

Across all categories, axes are treated as constraints on admissible
variation. No axis defines a direction of improvement or a desired end
state.

Their relevance is determined solely by their interaction with
thresholds, cycles, and continuity.
