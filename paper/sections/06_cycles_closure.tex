\section{Cycles and Closure}

\subsection{Cycles as a Condition for Persistence}

Within the Ontology of Continua, persistence over time is not assumed.
A continuum may satisfy instantaneous admissibility conditions while
failing to persist due to the absence of recurrent structural closure.

For the enterprise continuum $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$, persistence requires
the existence of a minimal set of recurrent cycles
$C_{\mathrm{EA}}$ that close over the internal time scale
$\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

These cycles are structural requirements.
They are not processes to be optimized, improved, redesigned, or
managed within the scope of the model.
Their role is solely to sustain the definability of the continuum over
time.

\subsection{Required Cycle Classes}

The Executable Theory Specification specifies a minimal set of required
cycle classes.
Each class denotes a category of structural closure rather than a
specific organizational or technical implementation.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Governance cycles}: recurrent decision, enforcement, and
        feedback structures required to maintain rule coherence.
  \item \textbf{Value cycles}: production, delivery, and consumption
        closures required to sustain enterprise identity.
  \item \textbf{Reproduction cycles}: mechanisms that regenerate
        capabilities, roles, or structural components.
  \item \textbf{Financial cycles}: inflow, allocation, and outflow
        closures required for material persistence.
  \item \textbf{Learning cycles}: recurrent updates of internal models
        in response to observed discrepancies.
\end{itemize}

The model does not prescribe how these closures are realized.
Only their existence as closed recurrent structures is required.

\subsection{Formal Notion of Closure}

Closure is defined structurally as the existence of recurrent paths in
the admissible state graph of the enterprise continuum.
Formally, closure requires that admissible trajectories form closed
paths over the internal time scale $\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

In \texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}, this condition is expressed as the existence
of graph-closed paths or recurrent patterns.
No assumptions are made regarding efficiency, speed, optimality, or
resource consumption of closure.

\subsection{Failure of Closure and Stability}

Failure of one or more required cycles does not necessarily imply
immediate termination of the continuum.
However, sustained absence of closure leads to violation of stability
conditions encoded in $\Theta_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

In such regimes, the continuum may transiently exist but cannot be
treated as persistent.
Diagnosis remains defined only until stability or termination thresholds
are crossed.

\subsection{Cycles Versus Processes}

Cycles must be distinguished from operational or managerial processes.
Processes may exist without forming closed loops, and cycles may be
realized through heterogeneous or distributed processes.

The model makes no claims about process design or execution.
It asserts only that structural closure, independent of implementation,
is required for persistence.

\subsection{Implications for Diagnosis}

The presence or absence of required cycles directly affects:
\begin{itemize}
  \item stability-related threshold components,
  \item the continuity measure $k_{\mathrm{EA}}$,
  \item the admissibility of continued diagnosis.
\end{itemize}

Cycles therefore constitute the formal link between instantaneous
admissibility and temporal persistence in the enterprise continuum.
