\section{Problem Statement and Structural Failure Modes}

\subsection{Limits of Conventional Enterprise Analysis}

Enterprise analysis is commonly conducted under a set of implicit
assumptions that are rarely made explicit.
Among the most prevalent are the assumptions that the enterprise
persists as a coherent object, that diagnostic statements remain
meaningful under arbitrary degradation, and that recovery is always, in
principle, attainable.

Although typically unstated, these assumptions shape both the
interpretation of observations and the decision to continue analysis
even when structural coherence is no longer demonstrable.
As a consequence, analytical output may be produced in regimes where
its validity is undefined rather than merely uncertain.

Within such approaches, failure is usually framed as a local deficiency
(e.g.\ governance gaps, execution errors, or strategic misalignment).
The structural conditions under which the enterprise itself remains a
well-defined object of analysis are seldom formalized.

\subsection{Enterprises as Conditional Objects}

Within the Ontology of Continua, an enterprise is not treated as an
unconditional object.
It is defined as a continuum whose existence, stability, and
diagnosability depend on explicit structural conditions.

The enterprise continuum $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ is admissible for diagnosis
only while the following conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the admissible state space $\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ is non-empty,
  \item the boundary $\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{EA}}$ remains well-defined,
  \item required recurrent cycles $C_{\mathrm{EA}}$ are present,
  \item observability and connectivity are sufficient to evaluate
        admissibility thresholds.
\end{itemize}

Violation of any of these conditions renders continued diagnosis
undefined within the model.

\subsection{Structural Failure Versus Performance Degradation}

$K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ draws a strict distinction between performance
degradation and structural failure.

Performance degradation refers to states in which the enterprise
continues to satisfy all existence and admissibility conditions, while
exhibiting reduced capability along one or more axes.
Structural failure, by contrast, denotes violations of the conditions
required for the enterprise to be treated as a continuum at all.

Only performance degradation admits continued diagnosis within
$K_{\mathrm{EA}}$.
Structural failure does not represent a poor outcome; it represents a
loss of definability.

\subsection{Classes of Structural Failure Modes}

Structural failure modes are formalized through threshold components
$f_k$ as specified in \texttt{ETS.K\_EA.v1.1}.
These components do not measure outcomes or performance.
They express violations of admissibility conditions.

The principal classes are:

\paragraph{Existence Failure ($f_{\text{exist}}$).}
The enterprise no longer maintains a non-empty admissible state space.
This may arise through legal dissolution, loss of organizational
identity, or complete loss of operational closure.

\paragraph{Stability Failure ($f_{\text{stab}}$).}
Recurrent cycles required for persistence cease to function.
The system may persist transiently, but cannot maintain itself over the
characteristic internal time scale $\tau_{\mathrm{EA}}$.

\paragraph{Inertial Failure ($f_{\text{inertia}}$).}
The enterprise remains structurally intact but is unable to transition
between admissible states without external intervention.
Diagnosis remains defined, but endogenous evolution is constrained.

\paragraph{Collapse Failure ($f_{\text{collapse}}$).}
Structural tension exceeds admissible bounds, leading to irreversible
loss of coherence.
Local cycles or axes may persist, but the enterprise as a whole no
longer satisfies stability conditions.

\paragraph{Termination Condition ($f_{\text{stop}}$).}
Conditions under which diagnosis must terminate regardless of other
indicators.
These include loss of observability and violations of compatibility
constraints imposed by parent continua.

\subsection{STOP as a Diagnostic Boundary}

STOP is defined as the highest-precedence diagnostic outcome.
When STOP conditions are met, the model explicitly forbids further
diagnosis.

This boundary is not heuristic.
It follows directly from the executable specification.
Any continuation of analysis beyond STOP would require assumptions not
contained in the ETS and is therefore outside the model.

\subsection{Implications for Diagnostic Integrity}

By making structural failure modes explicit, $K_{\mathrm{EA}}$ prevents
the silent extension of analysis beyond its domain of validity.

This does not assert that recovery is impossible, nor does it prescribe
any course of action.
It states only that, within the formal object defined by
$K_{\mathrm{EA}}$, diagnosis is undefined once STOP conditions are met.

The explicit separation between diagnosable degradation and
non-diagnosable structural failure is central to the integrity of the
enterprise continuum formulation.
