\section{Forbidden Operations}

\subsection{Normative Definition}
Within OPS-0, an operation is classified as \textbf{forbidden} if it violates any
\textbf{scope lock}, \textbf{diagnostic-only constraint}, \textbf{non-claim requirement},
or if its execution, interpretation, or continuation would cross a
\textbf{STOP / NO-GO boundary} as defined by this contract.

RUN-OPS0-01 \textbf{MUST NOT} define, enumerate, populate, or operationalize any forbidden-operation list.
Forbidden status in this run is established \textbf{exclusively} through contract-level prohibitions,
precedence rules, and registry bindings referenced elsewhere in OPS-0.

No forbidden operation may be partially executed, conditionally allowed, or treated as recoverable
under this contract.

\subsection{Registry Binding (Exclusive)}
The classification of forbidden operations is \textbf{normatively bound} to appendix contract registries (CT-xx),
which, in later runs, may enumerate explicitly disallowed operation classes and patterns.

In RUN-OPS0-01:
\begin{itemize}
  \item appendix registries \textbf{MUST} be referenced for structural completeness only;
  \item appendix registries \textbf{MUST NOT} be populated, evaluated, or interpreted;
  \item no forbidden status may be inferred outside explicit registry or contract prohibition.
\end{itemize}

Any operation not explicitly permitted by registry binding in a later run
and not explicitly prohibited by this contract
\textbf{MUST} still be treated as \emph{non-admissible} under the closed-world assumption.

\subsection{Precedence Rule (Hard)}
OPS-0 enforces a strict \textbf{prohibition precedence rule}.

In any case of ambiguity, overlap, or conflicting classification signals:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{forbidden status takes absolute precedence} over admissibility;
  \item the operation \textbf{MUST} be treated as forbidden;
  \item no clarification, disambiguation, or continuation is permitted within the same run.
\end{itemize}

If such a conflict is detected during evaluation or classification,
the tool \textbf{MUST} emit a terminal STOP in accordance with the applicable STOP registry binding
and terminate the run.
