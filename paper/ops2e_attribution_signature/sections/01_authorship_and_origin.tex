\section{Authorship \& Origin Declaration}

\subsection{Purpose (Normative)}
This section defines authorship, origin, and non-endorsement semantics for the OPS-2 Toolkit as an open diagnostic standard.
It establishes who authored and originated the standard, how contributors are acknowledged, and what is explicitly not implied by authorship or attribution.

This section is declarative only and introduces no rights, licenses, governance, or enforcement mechanisms.

\subsection{Authorship}
\paragraph{Primary Author / Originator.}
The OPS-2 Toolkit and its normative specifications (OPS-2A \ldots OPS-2E) originate from:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Alexander Yashin}\\
as part of the \textbf{EA-OC-EXTENSION / ESTRA} research and diagnostic framework.
\end{quote}
Authorship denotes intellectual origin and authorship of the standard text and structure, not ownership of implementations.

\subsection{Contributors}
Contributors may include reviewers, editors, commentators, and implementers providing feedback.
Unless explicitly stated otherwise, contributors do not become co-authors of the standard and do not assume responsibility for the standard's content.

Contribution does not imply endorsement, maintenance obligation, or authority.

\subsection{Origin of the Standard}
OPS-2 is an open diagnostic toolkit standard originating within:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{EA-OC-EXTENSION / ESTRA}\\
(Enterprise Systems Topology \& Risk Architecture)
\end{quote}
OPS-2 is defined as diagnostic-only, non-prescriptive, non-operational, and non-governing.
Its origin does not imply institutional backing, regulatory status, or recognition by a formal standardization body.

\subsection{Non-Endorsement Semantics (Critical)}
Authorship or attribution under OPS-2 must not be interpreted as endorsement of any implementation, certification or validation of results, approval of any usage context, agreement with conclusions drawn from diagnostic outputs, or assumption of liability or fitness for purpose.

\paragraph{Normative statement.}
Attribution to OPS-2 or its author does not imply endorsement, warranty, responsibility, or authority transfer under any circumstances.

\subsection{Separation of Standard vs.\ Implementation}
The OPS-2 standard defines diagnostic semantics and boundaries, but does not define implementations and does not control downstream usage.
Any implementation remains fully independent and carries its own responsibility and claims.

Implementations must not present themselves as \emph{official}, \emph{certified}, or \emph{approved} by the OPS-2 author or ESTRA.

\subsection{No Transfer of Authority}
Nothing in this declaration transfers decision authority, creates fiduciary responsibility, establishes supervisory roles, or enables representation on behalf of the author or ESTRA.
Authorship is not governance.
