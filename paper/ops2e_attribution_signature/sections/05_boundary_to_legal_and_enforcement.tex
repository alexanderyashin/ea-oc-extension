\section{Boundary to Legal and Enforcement Domains}

\subsection{Purpose (Normative)}
This section defines explicit boundaries between the OPS-2E attribution and signature layer and legal, regulatory, enforcement, certification, or governance domains.
Its purpose is to prevent misinterpretation of OPS-2E as a legal instrument, compliance regime, or enforceable standard.

\subsection{No Legal Advice (Explicit)}
OPS-2E does not provide legal advice, does not interpret laws, regulations, or licenses, and does not replace professional legal counsel.
Any legal interpretation of attribution, reuse, or derivative work is outside the scope of OPS-2E.

\subsection{No Licensing or Rights Grant}
OPS-2E does not grant licenses, restrict licenses, define intellectual property rights, or override applicable law.
Attribution rules defined here are declarative expectations, not legal instruments.

\subsection{No Enforcement or Policing}
OPS-2E defines no enforcement authority, compliance body, sanction mechanism, or dispute resolution process.
Non-conformance may affect credibility or transparency but does not trigger enforcement under OPS-2E.

\subsection{No Certification or Validation}
OPS-2E does not certify tools, reports, implementations, or outcomes.
It does not validate correctness, quality, or suitability, and does not approve or reject usage contexts.

Any claim of ``OPS-2-certified'', ``OPS-2-compliant'', or similar formulations is explicitly non-conforming.

\subsection{No Warranty or Fitness Claims}
OPS-2E provides no warranty and no guarantee of accuracy, completeness, or fitness for any purpose.
All use of OPS-2 is at the user's own responsibility.

\subsection{Boundary Statement (Normative Anchor)}
\begin{quote}
OPS-2E defines attribution and provenance expectations only.
It does not operate in legal, enforcement, certification, or governance domains.
Any interpretation beyond this boundary is invalid.
\end{quote}
