\section{Scope and Non-Claims}

\subsection{Diagnostic-only boundary}

This artefact defines the \emph{Engine Core} as a \textbf{diagnostic-only kernel}.
Its scope is strictly limited to:
(i) accepting a declared structural state snapshot,
(ii) evaluating that snapshot against a declared invariant set,
(iii) emitting a structural classification, and
(iv) irreversibly terminating evaluation when STOP conditions apply.

The Engine Core has \textbf{no causal influence} on the diagnosed system.
It must not be embedded or interpreted as an actuator, controller, optimizer,
governance mechanism, or decision authority.

\paragraph{Closed operation set (normative).}
The following operations exhaust the admissible behavior of the kernel:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{OBSERVE:} accept a read-only state snapshot;
  \item \textbf{EVALUATE:} evaluate declared invariants on that snapshot;
  \item \textbf{CLASSIFY:} map invariant satisfaction to a finite structural label;
  \item \textbf{STOP/HALT:} terminate evaluation irreversibly for the given case.
\end{itemize}

These operations denote \emph{structural roles}, not procedural steps.
No additional operations, phases, or control constructs exist inside the kernel.

\subsection{OPS-1 contract vs.\ toolkits (OPS-2)}

OPS-1 is an \textbf{architecture-and-contract layer}.
It defines invariants, prohibitions, authority, and validity conditions.
It is \textbf{not} usage-centered, tool-centered, or user-centered:
OPS-1 does not know users, workflows, commands, screens, or decisions.

Any toolkit surface (CLI/GUI/scripts/integrations) belongs to OPS-2 and is, by
definition, an \textbf{exchangeable consumer} of the OPS-1 contract.
OPS-2 may change without changing OPS-1; OPS-1 must not be redefined by OPS-2.

\paragraph{Binary compliance.}
OPS-1 compliance is \textbf{binary}: \emph{conformant} or \emph{non-ESTRA}.
A well-intended toolkit that introduces prohibited semantics is \textbf{not} ESTRA.

\paragraph{Non-normativity of toolkits.}
Toolkits are never normative. They must not \emph{create} new semantic degrees of
freedom, reinterpret STOP, or turn structural labels into prescriptions.
UX or product arguments do not justify any semantic change to OPS-1.

\paragraph{Auto-disqualification (boundary violation).}
Any representation of OPS-1 as a ``method'', ``tool'', ``framework'',
``best practice'', ``playbook'', or ``how-to'' is a category error.
If a consumer layer induces or implies ``you should do X'' from OPS-1 output,
that representation is \textbf{non-conformant} and therefore \textbf{non-ESTRA}.

\subsection{No-prescription / no-management claims}

This document makes \textbf{no prescriptive} and \textbf{no managerial} claims.

\paragraph{Explicit non-claims.}
The Engine Core does not:
\begin{itemize}
  \item recommend actions, remediations, or interventions;
  \item define roadmaps, sequences, or execution plans;
  \item set targets, KPIs, objectives, or success criteria;
  \item prioritize options or rank alternatives;
  \item allocate resources or define governance processes;
  \item provide best practices, frameworks, or transformation methodology.
\end{itemize}

Any process, facilitation, governance, or decision-making that consumes diagnostic
output is \textbf{external} to the Engine Core and must be handled by higher layers.

\subsection{What the engine does not infer}

The Engine Core must not infer missing structure or enrich semantics.
It operates solely on explicitly provided inputs.

\paragraph{Forbidden inference classes.}
The kernel does not:
\begin{itemize}
  \item infer intent, priorities, accountability, or organizational motives;
  \item infer causal narratives beyond invariant reference;
  \item forecast future states or simulate counterfactual scenarios;
  \item learn from history or adapt thresholds or rules;
  \item complete missing observability via assumptions or heuristics.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Structural determinacy (scope-limited).}
The only determinacy claim is internal:
for identical declared inputs (state snapshot, invariant set, thresholds),
the kernel yields the same classification and STOP decision.
This is not a claim about determinism of the world, the enterprise, or outcomes.

\paragraph{STOP as boundary, not signal.}
STOP is not advice, not an error, and not a recoverable warning.
It denotes a terminal boundary where further diagnostic evaluation is formally
non-admissible for the given case (see Section~\ref{sec:stop}).
