\section{Problem Statement}

\subsection{Why an Engine Core artefact exists}

In large-scale systems and organizations, diagnostic mechanisms are frequently
entangled with control, optimization, or governance processes. As a consequence,
diagnostic artefacts inherit implicit objectives, adaptive behavior, and
prescriptive semantics that obscure their epistemic status.

The Engine Core artefact exists to enforce a strict separation between
\emph{diagnosis} and \emph{intervention}. It defines a formally constrained
diagnostic kernel whose outputs are limited to structural classification and
irreversible STOP boundaries. By construction, the Engine Core cannot recommend
actions, optimize outcomes, or modify its own behavior.

This separation is not a design preference but a structural requirement.
Without an explicitly bounded diagnostic kernel, diagnostic results become
inseparable from the control or governance mechanisms that consume them, and
their validity cannot be assessed independently.

The Engine Core therefore functions as a stable diagnostic anchor. Higher-level
tooling may consume its outputs, but responsibility for interpretation,
decision-making, and action attribution remains strictly external to the kernel.

\subsection{What is explicitly out of scope}

PDF-4 intentionally excludes all concerns that would transform diagnosis into
control, prescription, or interpretation.

\paragraph{Out-of-scope domains.}
The Engine Core does not address:
\begin{itemize}
  \item decision-making, prioritization, or trade-off resolution;
  \item transformation planning, roadmaps, or execution sequencing;
  \item organizational roles, accountability, or governance structures;
  \item performance management, KPIs, or success criteria;
  \item user interaction, facilitation, or interpretive guidance.
\end{itemize}

These concerns may be handled by external artefacts or processes, but they must
not be embedded into the Engine Core itself.

\paragraph{Deliberate incompleteness.}
The Engine Core does not aim to be a complete system or organizational model.
Its incompleteness is structural, not incidental: the artefact derives its
diagnostic validity from the explicit refusal to encode control, optimization,
or prescriptive semantics.
