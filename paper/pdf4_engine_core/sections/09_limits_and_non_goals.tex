\section{Limits and Non-Goals}

This section defines the \textbf{explicit limits} of the Engine Core and
fixes what is intentionally excluded from PDF-4.
These limits are \textbf{constitutive}.
They define the boundary of admissible interpretation
for OPS-1 diagnostic semantics.

\subsection{Interpretation boundaries}

Diagnostic outputs produced by the Engine Core are
\textbf{structural classifications only}.
They express admissibility or non-admissibility
with respect to declared invariants
and terminal STOP conditions.

Any interpretation beyond invariant satisfaction
is \textbf{external} to the kernel
and has \textbf{no standing} within OPS-1.

\paragraph{No semantic lifting (prohibition).}
Consumers of diagnostic output \textbf{must not}
treat classifications or STOP as:
\begin{itemize}
  \item recommendations, guidance, or action directives;
  \item root-cause analyses or explanatory narratives;
  \item forecasts, predictions, or risk estimates;
  \item compliance judgments, managerial assessments,
        or performance evaluations;
  \item priorities, rankings, weights, targets,
        or any notion of ``what matters most''.
\end{itemize}

Any such interpretation requires additional assumptions,
evidence, or authority and is \textbf{excluded}
from the Engine Core boundary.

\subsection{OPS-1 vs.\ OPS-2 responsibility split}

OPS-1 is a \textbf{contract and authority layer}.
It fixes admissibility, invariants,
terminal STOP semantics,
determinism, reproducibility,
and provenance identity.

OPS-1 \textbf{does not} address usage,
presentation, tooling,
orchestration, or decision-making.

Any toolkit surface (CLI, GUI, scripts, integrations)
belongs to OPS-2 and is an
\textbf{exchangeable consumer} of OPS-1.
OPS-2 may display, transport,
or package results,
but it \textbf{must not} define,
reinterpret, operationalize,
aggregate, rank, or extend OPS-1 semantics.

\paragraph{Binary compliance (invariant).}
Compliance is \textbf{binary}:
conformant or \emph{non-ESTRA}.
Intent, usability goals,
product considerations,
or domain expectations
\textbf{do not} soften this boundary.

\paragraph{Auto-disqualification by category error.}
Any representation of OPS-1 as a
``method'', ``tool'',
``framework'', ``best practice'',
``playbook'', or ``how-to''
is a category error.
If any OPS-2 layer
(or its documentation)
implies ``you should do X''
from a classification
or from terminal STOP,
it introduces prescriptive semantics
and is therefore \textbf{non-ESTRA}.

\subsection{Known non-diagnostic cases}

The Engine Core \textbf{cannot}
produce admissible diagnostic results
when:
\begin{itemize}
  \item required observability is missing,
        indeterminate,
        or explicitly absent;
  \item invariants are inconsistent,
        underspecified,
        or contradictory;
  \item threshold objects are ambiguous,
        incompatible,
        or ill-defined;
  \item kernel invariants are violated;
  \item provenance completeness
        cannot be established;
  \item determinism or reproducibility,
        as architectural invariants,
        is violated.
\end{itemize}

In all such cases,
diagnostic admissibility itself
is \textbf{violated}
and terminal STOP \textbf{applies}.
STOP does \textbf{not} compensate
for these conditions;
it denotes their formal recognition.

\subsection{What requires external evidence or authority}

The following concerns
\textbf{necessarily require}
evidence, processes,
or authority \textbf{outside}
the Engine Core:
\begin{itemize}
  \item remediation planning
        or corrective action design;
  \item prioritization,
        sequencing,
        or trade-off resolution;
  \item assignment of organizational
        responsibility or accountability;
  \item economic, performance,
        or impact evaluation;
  \item governance, approval,
        or decision authority.
\end{itemize}

PDF-4 \textbf{explicitly declines}
to address these concerns
in order to preserve
diagnostic neutrality
and ontological integrity
of the kernel.

\subsection{Explicit exclusion of workflows and methods}

PDF-4 \textbf{does not} define
workflows, procedures,
methods, playbooks,
recipes, or execution instructions
of any kind.

The diagnostic operation graph
defined in PDF-4 exists
\textbf{solely} to fix
admissibility boundaries
and terminal STOP enforcement.
It \textbf{must not} be interpreted as:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a process model
        or workflow description;
  \item a decision tree
        or control structure;
  \item an execution sequence
        to be followed
        by agents or systems;
  \item a method for optimization,
        steering, intervention,
        or management.
\end{itemize}

Any interpretation of the operation graph
as a workflow, method,
or procedural guidance
is \textbf{non-admissible}
under OPS-1
and outside the declared
scope of PDF-4.

\subsection{Determinism and reproducibility are not process-negotiable}

OPS-1 fixes determinism
and reproducibility
as \textbf{architectural invariants}.
Accordingly, PDF-4 \textbf{does not}
define, recommend,
imply, or tolerate
any process-level mechanisms
intended to ``achieve'',
``improve'',
or ``enforce'' them.

If determinism or reproducibility
would require procedural controls
to hold,
the artefact is
\textbf{non-admissible}
under OPS-1
and produced outputs
are \textbf{invalid}.

\subsection{Forbidden extensions (auto-disqualifying)}

The following extension classes
are \textbf{explicitly forbidden}.
\textbf{If any is present,
the artefact is NOT ESTRA-conformant.}

\paragraph{Semantic control and steering.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item any optimization,
        recommendation,
        prioritization,
        ranking,
        weighting,
        scoring,
        targeting,
        or decision semantics;
  \item any control logic
        influencing or steering
        diagnostic outcomes;
  \item any feedback edge
        or adaptive branching
        affecting evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Learning and adaptation.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item any learning, fitting,
        calibration, adaptation,
        or tuning influencing
        invariants, thresholds,
        classification,
        provenance identity,
        admissibility,
        or terminal STOP;
  \item any use of historical cases
        to modify kernel behavior.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{STOP violation.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item any retry, recovery,
        bypass, continuation path,
        or ``soft STOP''
        after terminal STOP;
  \item any reinterpretation of STOP
        as warning, error,
        signal, advisory,
        or ``needs review''.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Non-determinism and provenance violation.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item any semantically relevant
        non-determinism;
  \item any partial, probabilistic,
        inferred, reconstructed,
        or narrative provenance;
  \item any post-hoc correction,
        repair, normalization,
        or retrofitting of evidence
        or diagnostic artefacts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Registry and evidence manipulation.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item mutable registries
        or evidence stores;
  \item overwriting, replacing,
        deleting, rewriting,
        or reordering evidence
        based on relevance
        or ``importance'';
  \item opaque preprocessing,
        hidden sampling,
        hidden filtering,
        or undisclosed
        selection criteria.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Renderer and reporting semantics (presentation-driven meaning).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item any visualization, UI,
        report, or default view
        that implies preference,
        importance, priority,
        ``severity'',
        ``criticality'',
        or ``health''
        from OPS-1 outputs;
  \item any ranking, top-$N$,
        ordering by ``risk'',
        ``impact'',
        or ``value'',
        heatmap semantics,
        traffic-light semantics,
        or highlight rules
        acting as a normative lens;
  \item any report text
        interpreting outputs
        as guidance,
        recommended action,
        or procedural next step.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Evidence import semantics (adapter-driven meaning).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item any import adapter
        that filters, cleans,
        normalizes, imputes,
        smooths, deduplicates
        by relevance,
        treats outliers,
        or otherwise transforms
        evidence
        in a semantics-bearing way;
  \item any adapter behavior
        where independent implementers
        ingest the same external evidence
        and obtain materially different
        semantics
        without an explicitly declared,
        reversible mapping rule.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Graded or stratified verification (gate-driven meaning).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item any ``quality levels'',
        ``maturity tiers'',
        or graded acceptance
        (e.g.\ pass-with-advice,
        bronze/silver/gold),
        regardless of naming;
  \item any gate output
        acting as a score,
        ranking,
        or preference signal
        rather than a strict
        binary predicate
        (pass/fail).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Witness packaging semantics (packaging-driven narrative).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item curated subsets,
        ``highlights'',
        ``executive packs'',
        ``most important artefacts'',
        or any selection
        or reordering
        intended to create
        narrative, weight,
        or reading guidance;
  \item any packaging
        that changes meaning
        by emphasizing,
        suppressing,
        or sequencing artefacts.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Binary auto-disqualification rule (invariant).}
If any extension can change
a reader's decision preference
without changing the underlying
computed artefacts,
it introduces normative semantics
and is therefore \textbf{non-ESTRA}.

Presence of any forbidden class
\textbf{immediately disqualifies}
the artefact from OPS-1
and ESTRA conformance,
regardless of tooling,
intent,
or external justification.
