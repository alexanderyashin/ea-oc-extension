\section{Limits and Non-Goals}

This section defines the \textbf{explicit limits} of the Engine Core and clarifies
what is intentionally not addressed by PDF-4. These limits are constitutive: they
define the boundary of admissible interpretation for OPS-1 diagnostic semantics.

\subsection{Interpretation boundaries}

Diagnostic outputs produced by the Engine Core are \emph{structural
classifications only}. They express admissibility or non-admissibility with
respect to declared invariants.

Any interpretation beyond invariant satisfaction is external to the kernel.

\paragraph{No semantic lifting.}
Consumers of diagnostic output must not treat classifications as:
\begin{itemize}
  \item recommendations or action guidance;
  \item root-cause analyses or explanatory narratives;
  \item forecasts, predictions, or risk estimates;
  \item compliance judgments or managerial assessments.
\end{itemize}

Such interpretations require additional assumptions, evidence, or authority that
are explicitly outside the Engine Core boundary.

\subsection{Known non-diagnostic cases}

The Engine Core cannot produce admissible diagnostic results when:
\begin{itemize}
  \item required observability is missing or indeterminate;
  \item invariants are inconsistent, underspecified, or contradictory;
  \item threshold objects are ambiguous or incompatible;
  \item kernel invariants are violated;
  \item provenance completeness cannot be established;
  \item determinism or reproducibility (as architectural invariants) is violated.
\end{itemize}

In these cases, diagnostic admissibility itself is violated and STOP applies. STOP
does not compensate for these conditions; it denotes their formal recognition.

\subsection{What requires external evidence or authority}

The following concerns necessarily require evidence, processes, or authority
\emph{outside} the Engine Core:
\begin{itemize}
  \item remediation planning or corrective action design;
  \item prioritization, sequencing, or trade-off resolution;
  \item assignment of organizational responsibility or accountability;
  \item economic, performance, or impact evaluation;
  \item governance, approval, or decision authority.
\end{itemize}

PDF-4 explicitly declines to address these concerns in order to preserve the
diagnostic neutrality and ontological integrity of the kernel.

\subsection{Explicit exclusion of workflows and methods}

PDF-4 does not define workflows, procedures, methods, playbooks, or execution
recipes of any kind.

The diagnostic operation graph defined in PDF-4 exists solely to fix admissibility
boundaries and terminal STOP enforcement. It must not be interpreted as:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a process model or workflow description;
  \item a decision tree or control structure;
  \item an execution sequence to be followed by agents or systems;
  \item a method for optimization, steering, or intervention.
\end{itemize}

Any interpretation of the operation graph as a workflow, method, or procedural
guidance is non-admissible under OPS-1 and falls outside the declared scope of
PDF-4.

\subsection{Determinism and reproducibility are not negotiated by process}

OPS-1 fixes determinism and reproducibility as \textbf{architectural invariants}.
Therefore, PDF-4 explicitly does not define, recommend, or imply any process-level
mechanisms to ``achieve'' or ``improve'' them.

If determinism or reproducibility would require procedural controls to hold, the
artefact is non-admissible under OPS-1 and produced outputs are invalid.

\subsection{Verbotsspace: registry/evidence manipulation (architectural)}

OPS-1 fixes registry and evidence as architectural invariants. Therefore, the
Engine Core explicitly \textbf{does not} define, permit, or imply any of the
following:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Mutable registry:} editing, rewriting, replacing, or deleting
        registry entries.
  \item \textbf{Evidence overwrite or replacement:} any ability to overwrite,
        replace, or delete evidence artefacts after creation.
  \item \textbf{Evidence normalization/cleaning:} any transformation intended to
        ``clean'', ``normalize'', or otherwise alter evidence content.
  \item \textbf{Aggregation with meaning change:} any aggregation step that
        modifies the semantic content of evidence.
  \item \textbf{Post-hoc correction:} any after-the-fact repair of evidence or
        registry meant to retrofit consistency or improve appearance.
  \item \textbf{Opaque preprocessing:} any uninspectable transformation that
        breaks traceability.
  \item \textbf{Hidden sampling/filtering:} any undisclosed selection/exclusion
        that prevents complete provenance.
\end{itemize}

If any of the above is present or required, OPS-1 conformance is violated and
produced outputs are non-admissible under the declared scope of PDF-4.
